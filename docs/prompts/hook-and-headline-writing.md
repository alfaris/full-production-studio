# Hook and Headline Writer

Create hooks and headlines that stop the scroll and compel action using systematic frameworks, not guesswork.

## Purpose

80% of content performance comes from the hook. This skill provides the frameworks to craft headlines that capture attention and drive engagement - for any industry, any platform, any content type.

**Core Philosophy:** Generate volume (10+ options), then select best using proven criteria. The first option is rarely the best option.

## When to Use This Skill

- Writing newsletter subject lines that get opens
- Crafting social media thread hooks that stop the scroll
- Creating LinkedIn post first lines that compel "see more"
- Optimizing headlines for blog posts or articles
- Generating multiple hook variations for A/B testing

**Pairs well with:** `anti-ai-writing` (for humanizing headlines), voice style skills (for brand-specific tone)

---

## Key Principles

### The 3-Sentence Rule for Social Hooks
- Maximum 3 sentences for social media hooks
- First sentence MUST be short
- Each sentence's goal: get reader to the next

### The 25% Rule
How to allocate time on any content:
- 25% ideation (identifying what to say)
- 25% hook/headline (crafting how to say it)
- 50% body content

### Volume → Selection
- Generate 10+ variations minimum
- Select best using systematic criteria (not gut feel)
- The first option is rarely the best option

### Story = Problem + Goal + Path
This equation equals value. Use it to identify what transformation your hook promises.

## The 3-Phase Workflow

### Phase 1: Generate Hook Ideas (Ideation)

**Goal:** Identify the core benefit, transformation, or insight your hook will promise

#### Step 1: Use the "Start Questioning" Method

Ask who/what/where/when/why/how about your topic. Then dig deeper by asking multiple times on the same answer.

**Example:**
- **Topic:** Productivity systems
- **What?** "What is the system?" → Time-blocking with deep work periods
- **Why?** "Why does it work?" → Eliminates context switching
- **Why (deeper)?** "Why does that matter?" → You get 3x output in half the time
- **How?** "How do people fail at this?" → They schedule too many blocks
- **Impact angle discovered:** "The counterintuitive key to time-blocking: schedule LESS"

**Quality Check:**
- [ ] Asked who/what/where/when/why/how
- [ ] Dug deeper at least 2-3 times on promising angles
- [ ] Identified a specific, impactful aspect (not just surface-level info)

#### Step 2: Identify the Transformation

Use the **Story = Problem + Goal + Path** framework:

- **Problem:** What struggle/pain does the audience face?
- **Goal:** What result do they want?
- **Path:** What's the journey between them?

**Example:**
- **Problem:** Knowledge workers feel busy but unproductive
- **Goal:** Get meaningful work done and leave on time
- **Path:** Discover the 3-block system that eliminates busywork

**Your hook should promise movement along this path.**

#### Step 3: Identify Headline Elements

Extract these 6 elements from your content:
1. **Problem** - What pain/struggle exists?
2. **Goal** - What result does reader want?
3. **Benefit** - What will their life look like after?
4. **Concept** - What idea/principle applies?
5. **Example** - What story/data illustrates this?
6. **Process** - What steps lead to the result?

You'll combine these in Phase 2 to generate variations.

#### Step 4: "Become the Reader" Check

Before moving to Phase 2, review from audience perspective:
- **Ask:** "What's in it for me?"
- **Check:** Is this too abstract? (If you can't picture it, neither can they)
- **Verify:** Would I stop scrolling for this?

---

### Phase 2: Select Hook Type & Formula

**Goal:** Match your idea to proven frameworks that amplify it best

#### The Headline Element Combination System

**Combine 2 of the 6 elements** to create headline structure:

| Combination | Formula | Example |
|-------------|---------|---------|
| **Problem + Process** | "How to [Solve Problem] in [Steps]" | "How to Overcome Writer's Block in 5 Steps" |
| **Benefit + Timeframe** | "[Achieve Benefit] in [Time]" | "Double Your Productivity in 30 Days" |
| **Concept + Target Audience** | "[Concept] for [Specific People]" | "Minimalism for Busy Professionals" |
| **Goal + Benefit** | "[Achieve Goal] to [Get Benefit]" | "Build an Audience to Earn Money While You Sleep" |
| **Example + Process** | "[Specific Result] using [Method]" | "How I Hit 100K Followers Using 3 Simple Rules" |
| **Problem + Benefit** | "Stop [Problem] and Start [Benefit]" | "Stop Trading Time for Money and Start Building Passive Income" |

**How to use:**
1. Review your 6 elements from Phase 1
2. Try 5-10 different combinations
3. Select 2-3 that feel most powerful for your concept

#### Hook Type Selection

Choose the hook type that best matches your content goal:

**1. Curiosity Hooks** (create information gap)
- State what and why, leave out how
- State start and end, leave out middle
- Make bold claim, leave out data/proof

**Examples:**
- "I turned 33 today. I weighed in at 337lbs this week. Here's what happened and how I'll lose 140lbs in the next 18 months:"
- "10 years in SEO. Here's what every new marketer should know:"

---

**2. Transformation Hooks** (show before → after)
- Personal transformation stories
- Skill development timelines
- Business/financial progress

**Formula:** "[Before State] → [After State]. Here's how:"

**Examples:**
- "100 followers when I started. Last week, hit 10k. Here's how I did it:"
- "I quit my 9-5 in 6 months. The key? Mastering one high-income skill."

---

**3. Authority Hooks** (leverage expertise/results)
- Share years of experience
- Highlight specific achievements
- Borrow authority from others

**Formula:** "[Credentials/Results]. Here's what [audience] should know:"

**Examples:**
- "I've built and sold 3 startups. Here are 17 learnings for your entrepreneurial journey:"
- "I coached 12 clients to $40,000/month. If you want to hit $10K/month, read this:"

---

**4. Problem Callout Hooks** (identify pain immediately)
- Name the specific struggle
- Call out the mistake/gap
- Challenge common practice

**Formula:** "Are you making these [number] [topic] mistakes?"

**Examples:**
- "98.9% of people are still unaware of ChatGPT's full potential. Here are 10 advanced prompts:"
- "The costly mistake 90% of writers make"

---

**5. Target Callout Hooks** (speak directly to specific audience)
- Name the audience explicitly
- Address their specific context

**Formula:** "[Audience name]... [Action/insight they need]"

**Examples:**
- "Digital Writers... Stop committing these 4 headline mistakes"
- "Attention Founders: Your landing page is costing you customers"

---

**6. Bold Claim Hooks** (pattern interrupt)
- Make counterintuitive statement
- Challenge conventional wisdom
- Present surprising data

**Formula:** "[Surprising/controversial claim]. Here's why:"

**Examples:**
- "AI will create millionaires. Here's how you can be part of it:"
- "Forget everything you know about headline writing"

#### Apply Sticky Sentence Techniques

Enhance your hook using these literary devices:

**Alliteration** - Same starting sounds
- "Specificity is the secret"
- "The best jobs are neither decreed nor degreed"

**Symmetry** - Parallel structure
- "Read for awareness. Write for understanding."
- "It's not 10,000 hours. It's 10,000 iterations."

**Contrast** - Opposing ideas
- "To be everywhere is to be nowhere"
- "Be clear, not clever. Concise, not complex."

**Rhyme** - Similar ending sounds
- "Tell a story or lose your glory"

**Rhythm** - Pleasing cadence
- "When you can't wait to share it, they can't help but read it"

**Combining techniques:** For maximum stickiness, combine multiple
- "Anger prepares us to fight. Fear prepares us to flee." (Symmetry + Alliteration)

See `references/sticky-sentence-techniques.md` for detailed examples.

#### 15 Proven Headline Formulas

1. **How-to:** "How to [Achieve Result] in [Timeframe]"
2. **List:** "[Number] Ways to [Achieve Goal]"
3. **Question:** "Are You Making These [Number] Mistakes?"
4. **Curiosity-gap:** "The Surprising Truth About [Topic]"
5. **Benefit-driven:** "[Do This] to [Get Benefit]"
6. **Authority Secret:** "[Expert's] Secret to [Result]"
7. **Without Formula:** "Do [Great Thing] without [Thing They Hate]"
8. **Weird Trick:** "1 Weird Trick for [Result] with [Topic]"
9. **Quick and Easy:** "A Quick and Easy Way to [Solve Problem]"
10. **The Art Of:** "The Art of [Topic]"
11. **X Ways to Ruin:** "[Number] Ways to Ruin [Topic]"
12. **Name Your Reader:** "[Reader Type] + [Topic]"
13. **Teaser Introduction:** "[Number] proven strategies to [result]: (thread)"
14. **Essential Knowledge:** "[Number] tools every [profession] should know"
15. **Time-Saving Experience:** "I've tested [number]+ [things]. Save yourself [time] with these top picks"

See `references/headline-formulas-library.md` for full examples.

### Phase 3: Optimize & Select Best

**Goal:** Generate 10+ variations and select the best using systematic criteria

#### Step 1: Generate Volume (10+ Variations)

Using different combinations from Phase 2:
- Try 3-4 different hook types
- Apply 2-3 different headline formulas
- Test with/without sticky sentence techniques
- Vary element combinations

**Minimum output:** 10 hook variations

**Example for "Productivity System" content:**

1. "I wasted 3 years on productivity hacks. Then I found the one that actually works."
2. "Why the most productive people schedule LESS (not more)"
3. "10 years optimizing workflows. Here's what every knowledge worker should know:"
4. "The 3-block system: How I 3x'd my output while working fewer hours"
5. "Are you making these 3 time-blocking mistakes? (Most people make #2)"
6. "From 12-hour days to leaving at 5pm. Here's what changed:"
7. "The counterintuitive productivity hack: do less, achieve more"
8. "Knowledge workers: Stop trying to be busy. Start trying to be effective."
9. "I've tested 50+ productivity systems. Save yourself 3 years with these findings:"
10. "Forget time management. The best workers manage energy instead."

#### Step 2: Apply the 10 Commandments of Engagement

Optimize your top 5-7 variations using this checklist:

1. **Numbers and Statistics**
   - [ ] Uses specific numbers (not "many" or "several")
   - [ ] Includes percentages, dollar amounts, or timeframes

2. **Negativity Bias**
   - [ ] Highlights potential loss or mistake
   - [ ] Warns of consequences

3. **Pattern Interrupt**
   - [ ] Challenges common belief
   - [ ] Surprises or contradicts expectations

4. **Target Callout**
   - [ ] Directly addresses specific audience
   - [ ] Names them explicitly when appropriate

5. **Problem Callout**
   - [ ] Identifies common pain point immediately
   - [ ] Names the struggle specifically

6. **Confidence and Conviction**
   - [ ] Uses strong, assertive language
   - [ ] Eliminates hedge words (maybe, might, could)

7. **Aesthetics**
   - [ ] Visually clean and scannable
   - [ ] Line breaks in right places

8. **Potential Benefit**
   - [ ] Clearly states what reader will gain
   - [ ] Focuses on outcome, not just process

9. **Social Proof**
   - [ ] Leverages authority or popularity when relevant
   - [ ] References results, credentials, or validation

10. **Warning and Caution**
    - [ ] Creates urgency or importance
    - [ ] Suggests reader might be missing out

**Aim for 4-6 commandments per hook** for strong performance.

#### Step 3: Test with The 4 U's

Evaluate your top 3-5 options:

1. **Useful** - Does it offer clear value?
2. **Urgent** - Does it compel immediate action/attention?
3. **Unique** - Does it stand out from similar content?
4. **Ultra-specific** - Is it concrete (not vague)?

**Example of all 4 U's working:**
"7 Little-Known SEO Tricks to Boost Your Traffic by 204% in Just 30 Days"

- ✅ Useful: SEO tricks that boost traffic
- ✅ Urgent: "Just 30 days" creates timeframe
- ✅ Unique: "Little-known" implies non-obvious
- ✅ Ultra-specific: "204%" and "7 tricks" are concrete

**Quality threshold:** Score 3/4 or 4/4 on the U's.

#### Step 4: Final Selection Using Anatomy Criteria

Rate each finalist 1-5 on:

1. **Clarity** - Main point immediately clear?
2. **Specificity** - Concrete information or promise?
3. **Urgency** - Compels immediate action/interest?
4. **Uniqueness** - Stands out from competitors?
5. **Relevance** - Aligns with target audience's interests?

**Minimum passing score:** 20/25 total
**Aim for:** 22-25/25 for high performance

#### Step 5: "Become the Reader" Final Check

Before finalizing:
- [ ] **"What's in it for me?"** - Is the benefit crystal clear?
- [ ] **Too abstract?** - Can I picture what this promises?
- [ ] **Would I stop scrolling?** - Honest answer
- [ ] **Does it make sense standalone?** - Or does it need more context?

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

### Ideation Issues
❌ **Surface-level questioning** - Stopping at first "why"
❌ **Too abstract** - Can't visualize what you're promising
❌ **Missing transformation** - No clear before → after

### Formula Issues
❌ **First-match bias** - Using first formula that fits
❌ **Formula drift** - Abandoning structure mid-hook
❌ **Overcomplication** - Trying to say too much

### Optimization Issues
❌ **Skipping volume** - Writing 2-3 instead of 10+
❌ **Hedge words** - "Maybe," "might," "could" (kills conviction)
❌ **Generic promises** - "Boost productivity" vs "Double your output in 30 days"

### Selection Issues
❌ **Gut-based selection** - Picking favorite without criteria
❌ **Ignoring reader perspective** - Benefit unclear
❌ **Clickbait** - Promise you can't fulfill (destroys trust)

# 10 Commandments of Engagement Checklist

A systematic checklist for optimizing hooks and headlines based on proven psychological principles.

## How to Use This Checklist

**Purpose:** Evaluate and improve your top 5-7 hook variations before final selection

**Workflow:**
1. Generate 10+ hook variations (Phase 2 of main skill)
2. Select top 5-7 based on frameworks used
3. Run each through this checklist
4. Score: Count how many commandments each hook applies (aim for 4-6)
5. Optimize: Add missing commandments where natural
6. Select: Choose hook with highest score + best fit

**Important:** You don't need all 10 in every hook. Aim for 4-6. More than 7 risks feeling manipulative.

## Commandment 1: Numbers and Statistics

### Principle
Use specific numbers to add credibility and reduce vagueness

### Why It Works
- Brain processes numbers faster than words
- Specificity implies accuracy and research
- Concrete vs abstract (easier to visualize)
- Creates curiosity ("Why that specific number?")

### Checklist Questions
- [ ] Does the hook include a specific number?
- [ ] Are percentages, dollar amounts, or timeframes included?
- [ ] Have I avoided vague words like "many," "some," "lots"?
- [ ] Is the number realistic and accurate?

### Examples

**❌ Without numbers:**
- "Many businesses are using this strategy now"
- "Lots of people are making this mistake"
- "Save time with these tips"

**✅ With numbers:**
- "1.5 million businesses are using this strategy now"
- "90% of new founders make this mistake"
- "Save 100+ hours with these 7 tips"

### Types of Numbers to Use
- Percentages: "76% satisfaction rate"
- Dollar amounts: "$12,000 saved per year"
- Timeframes: "In 90 days"
- Quantities: "7 strategies"
- Specific results: "From 100 to 10,000 followers"
- Ratios: "1 person, 8 clients"

### When to Skip
- Number would be misleading or inaccurate
- Context is purely qualitative
- Too many numbers create clutter

---

## Commandment 2: Negativity Bias

### Principle
Highlight potential losses, mistakes, or negative consequences

### Why It Works
- Humans are wired to avoid loss more than seek gain
- Fear of missing out (FOMO) is powerful motivator
- Mistakes are more memorable than successes
- Creates urgency ("I need to fix this")

### Checklist Questions
- [ ] Does the hook warn of a potential loss?
- [ ] Are consequences of inaction clear?
- [ ] Does it identify what reader might be missing?
- [ ] Is the fear legitimate (not manipulative)?

### Examples

**❌ Positive framing only:**
- "How to succeed at freelancing"
- "Great tips for productivity"
- "Build a better business"

**✅ Negativity bias applied:**
- "The costly mistake 90% of freelancers make"
- "What you're missing about deep work"
- "Stop doing this before it kills your business"

### Ways to Apply
- **Mistake focus:** "Are you making this [X] mistake?"
- **Loss aversion:** "What [inaction] is costing you"
- **Warning:** "Before you [action], read this"
- **Missing out:** "What [group] doesn't want you to know"
- **Consequence:** "Why [common practice] is killing [goal]"

### When to Skip
- Content is purely inspirational
- Audience is already anxious (don't add more fear)
- Loss framing would be manipulative

### Balance with Positivity
Don't make everything negative. Best hooks often pair:
- Negative hook + positive solution
- "Stop [problem]. Start [solution]."

---

## Commandment 3: Pattern Interrupt

### Principle
Challenge common beliefs, practices, or expectations

### Why It Works
- Breaks through scrolling/scanning autopilot
- Creates cognitive dissonance ("Wait, what?")
- Signals non-obvious content
- Makes reader pause and reconsider

### Checklist Questions
- [ ] Does the hook challenge a common assumption?
- [ ] Would it make someone stop mid-scroll?
- [ ] Is it surprising or counterintuitive?
- [ ] Does it contradict conventional wisdom?

### Examples

**❌ Expected/conventional:**
- "Why you should plan your content calendar"
- "How to grow your audience effectively"
- "The importance of credentials"

**✅ Pattern interrupt:**
- "Forget everything you know about content strategy"
- "The less you plan, the more you ship"
- "Why the best founders have zero traditional credentials"

### Ways to Apply
- **Forget everything:** "Forget what you know about [topic]"
- **Opposite claim:** "The less [X], the more [Y]"
- **Surprising stat:** "98% don't know [surprising fact]"
- **Challenge authority:** "[Experts] are wrong about [topic]"
- **Reverse conventional wisdom:** "Why [common practice] actually hurts"

### Types of Pattern Interrupts

**1. Reversal:**
"The less you post, the more you grow"

**2. Negation:**
"Stop tracking vanity metrics. Start tracking revenue."

**3. Paradox:**
"The hardest path is often the easiest"

**4. Contradiction:**
"My business grew 10x when I started working 4 hours a day"

### When to Skip
- Content doesn't actually challenge assumptions
- You're being contrarian just for attention
- The interrupt would confuse rather than intrigue

---

## Commandment 4: Target Callout

### Principle
Directly address your specific audience by name or situation

### Why It Works
- Immediate self-selection ("This is for me")
- Increases relevance perception
- Cuts through noise for niche audiences
- Shows you understand them specifically

### Checklist Questions
- [ ] Does the hook name the target audience?
- [ ] Would readers immediately know if it's for them?
- [ ] Is the audience specific (not just "entrepreneurs" or "people")?
- [ ] Does it reference their unique context?

### Examples

**❌ Generic audience:**
- "Tips for better productivity"
- "How to grow a business"
- "Advice for professionals"

**✅ Target callout:**
- "Solopreneurs: Stop trading time for money"
- "Burned-out corporate employees: Here's your exit plan"
- "For creators considering going full-time:"

### Ways to Apply

**1. Direct address:**
"[Audience name]: [Message]"
- "Remote workers: The productivity system that actually works"

**2. Situation callout:**
"If you're [specific situation], [message]"
- "If your business is stuck at $10K/month, read this"

**3. Pain point specificity:**
"For [audience] struggling with [specific problem]"
- "For freelancers drowning in client work"

**4. Identity-based:**
"[Role/identity] need to know this:"
- "Senior developers who secretly hate managing people"

### Levels of Specificity

**Too broad:**
- "Entrepreneurs" (which entrepreneurs?)
- "Marketers" (what kind? what context?)

**Good:**
- "Solo consultants"
- "First-time founders"

**Very specific (often best):**
- "Solo consultants in their first 90 days"
- "Burned-out agency owners considering going solo"

### When to Skip
- Content applies to everyone equally
- Being too specific would exclude valuable readers
- Platform/context makes audience obvious

---

## Commandment 5: Problem Callout

### Principle
Identify the specific pain point, struggle, or obstacle immediately

### Why It Works
- Shows you understand their struggle
- Creates instant relatability
- Signals relevant solution incoming
- Pain is more motivating than gain initially

### Checklist Questions
- [ ] Does the hook name a specific problem?
- [ ] Would readers recognize this pain point?
- [ ] Is it concrete (not vague like "doing better")?
- [ ] Does it tap into real frustration?

### Examples

**❌ Vague or no problem:**
- "Tips for business growth"
- "How to improve marketing"
- "Better productivity approaches"

**✅ Problem callout:**
- "Tired of posting daily with zero engagement?"
- "Drowning in tools you never use?"
- "Spending $15K/month on ads with no ROI?"

### Ways to Apply

**1. Question format:**
"Tired of [specific problem]?"
- "Tired of endless meetings that accomplish nothing?"

**2. Pain statement:**
"[Problem] is killing [goal]"
- "Perfectionism is killing your productivity"

**3. Struggle acknowledgment:**
"Struggling with [specific issue]?"
- "Struggling to find clients who pay what you're worth?"

**4. Frustration naming:**
"The frustrating truth about [problem]"
- "The frustrating truth about 'passive income'"

### Problem Specificity Levels

**Too vague:**
- "Having trouble?"
- "Need help?"

**Good:**
- "Struggling with sales?"
- "Content not converting?"

**Very specific (often best):**
- "Spending 3 hours on emails that get no replies?"
- "Your 'viral' post got 10 likes from other creators?"

### Problem + Solution Pattern
Best hooks name problem, then promise solution:
- "Drowning in tasks? Here's the simple filter I use:"
- "Clients ghosting you? This changed everything:"

### When to Skip
- Content is aspirational/inspirational (not problem-focused)
- Problem would be offensive or insensitive
- Audience doesn't identify with having a "problem"

## Commandment 6: Confidence and Conviction

### Principle
Use strong, assertive language; eliminate hedge words

### Why It Works
- Confidence is persuasive
- Certainty reduces decision friction
- Hedge words signal doubt
- Reader wants decisive guidance, not maybes

### Checklist Questions
- [ ] Have I eliminated hedge words (maybe, might, could, possibly)?
- [ ] Do I speak in absolutes when accurate?
- [ ] Is the language assertive, not tentative?
- [ ] Would I say this with confidence to a friend?

### Examples

**❌ Hedging/uncertain:**
- "This might help you grow faster"
- "Possibly the best approach"
- "Could be useful for scaling"
- "I think maybe this works"

**✅ Confident/convicted:**
- "This is how to grow faster"
- "The best approach for scaling"
- "Use this for breakthrough results"
- "This works. Here's why:"

### Hedge Words to Eliminate

**Remove these:**
- Maybe, might, could, possibly, probably, perhaps
- I think, I believe, in my opinion (when unnecessary)
- Sort of, kind of, somewhat, fairly
- Try to, attempt to (just say "do")

**Replace with:**
- Is, will, does, works
- This is how, here's why, the truth is
- Simply, just, exactly

### Conviction Techniques

**1. Speak in absolutes (when accurate):**
- Not: "This could increase revenue"
- But: "This increases revenue"

**2. Use declarative statements:**
- Not: "I think hustle culture might not work"
- But: "Hustle culture doesn't work. Here's what does:"

**3. Command/imperative voice:**
- Not: "You might want to try..."
- But: "Do this:"

**4. Remove qualifiers:**
- Not: "This is a relatively simple approach"
- But: "This is simple"

### When to Add Nuance
- Legal/medical topics requiring disclaimers
- Complex topics needing context
- When absolute claim would be dishonest

### Confidence ≠ Arrogance
**Confident:** "This is the system that works"
**Arrogant:** "This is the only system that works, everyone else is wrong"

---

## Commandment 7: Aesthetics

### Principle
Ensure visual appeal and readability

### Why It Works
- Visual processing happens before reading
- Clean formatting signals quality
- Scannable structure invites reading
- Wall of text triggers avoidance

### Checklist Questions
- [ ] Is the hook visually clean?
- [ ] Are line breaks in the right places?
- [ ] Does it look easy to read at a glance?
- [ ] Would I stop scrolling based on how this looks?

### Examples

**❌ Poor aesthetics:**
```
I've spent 10 years building businesses and I've learned so much about what works and what doesn't work and I want to share the top 7 strategies that actually made a difference in our growth journey.
```
(Wall of text, no breaks, one sentence)

**✅ Good aesthetics:**
```
10 years building businesses.

Here are the 7 strategies that actually worked:
```
(Short lines, clear breaks, scannable)

### Formatting Rules

**For Social Media Hooks (X, LinkedIn, Facebook):**

1. **First line must be short** (5-12 words ideal)
2. **Use line breaks between sentences**
3. **Max 3 sentences for hook**
4. **Avoid ALL CAPS** (except strategic emphasis)
5. **Use emojis sparingly** (1-2 max, if at all)

**For Newsletter Subject Lines:**

1. **35-50 characters ideal** (mobile preview)
2. **No line breaks** (won't work in subject lines)
3. **Clear even when truncated**

**For Headlines:**

1. **One line when possible**
2. **Use title case or sentence case consistently**
3. **Avoid punctuation clusters** (!!!, ???)

### Visual Hierarchy

**Good pattern:**
```
[Short hook line]

[Context sentence]

[Promise/benefit]
```

**Bad pattern:**
```
[Everything crammed together without breaks and no visual hierarchy making it hard to scan or know where to focus attention]
```

### Whitespace Matters
More whitespace = easier to read = higher engagement

**Compare:**

Cramped:
```
10 years consulting. 5 startups. 150 clients. Here's what worked:
```

Spacious:
```
10 years consulting.
5 startups.
150 clients.

Here's what worked:
```

The spacious version performs better on social media.

### Platform-Specific Aesthetics

**X/Twitter:**
- Very short first line (hook)
- Generous line breaks
- Emoji at end of thread signal (🧵)

**LinkedIn:**
- First 2 lines show in feed (must work standalone)
- Professional formatting
- Line breaks for readability

**Facebook:**
- Can be denser (platform shows more)
- Questions work well
- Image + text combo ideal

### When to Skip
Subject lines and contexts where formatting doesn't display (plain text email, etc.)

## Commandment 8: Potential Benefit

### Principle
Clearly state what the reader will gain

### Why It Works
- Answers "What's in it for me?" immediately
- Reduces uncertainty about value
- Benefit > Feature (people care about outcomes)
- Clear ROI compels click

### Checklist Questions
- [ ] Is the benefit crystal clear?
- [ ] Does it focus on outcome, not just process?
- [ ] Can reader visualize their life after?
- [ ] Is benefit specific and tangible?

### Examples

**❌ Unclear/no benefit:**
- "Things about productivity"
- "Business information"
- "Growth strategies"

**✅ Clear benefit:**
- "How to cut your workweek by 20 hours"
- "The system that ended our sales slump forever"
- "7 tools that gave me 10 hours back per week"

### Benefit Types

**1. Time saved:**
- "Save 100 hours of research"
- "Cut planning time in half"

**2. Money saved/earned:**
- "Save $12,000/year"
- "How I earn $30K/month with 8 clients"

**3. Problem solved:**
- "End the feast-or-famine cycle"
- "Finally get consistent leads"

**4. Outcome achieved:**
- "Clients who actually pay on time"
- "From hating sales to closing deals confidently"

**5. Stress reduced:**
- "Stop feeling overwhelmed"
- "The guilt-free approach to growth"

**6. Transformation:**
- "From burned out to thriving"
- "Struggling creator to full-time income"

### Feature → Benefit Translation

**Feature:** "Automated email sequences"
**Benefit:** "Wake up to new sales without lifting a finger"

**Feature:** "Small group coaching"
**Benefit:** "Get help the moment you're stuck"

**Feature:** "Flexible schedule"
**Benefit:** "Work when you want, not when you have to"

### Specificity Levels

**Vague benefit:**
- "Better results"
- "Improved performance"
- "More success"

**Specific benefit:**
- "2x your close rate in 6 months"
- "From constant hustle to 4-hour workdays"
- "6-figure income with no employees"

### When to Skip
- Benefit is so obvious it's redundant
- Content is entertaining (not utility-focused)
- Audience reads for identity/philosophy (not outcomes)

## Commandment 9: Social Proof

### Principle
Leverage authority, popularity, or third-party validation

### Why It Works
- Reduces risk perception
- Borrowed credibility
- "If it worked for them..." (vicarious success)
- Authority shortcut to trust

### Checklist Questions
- [ ] Does the hook reference credible authority?
- [ ] Are results/credentials mentioned?
- [ ] Is third-party validation included?
- [ ] Would this build trust with skeptical reader?

### Examples

**❌ No social proof:**
- "How to grow a business"
- "Tips for freelancing"
- "Marketing strategies that work"

**✅ With social proof:**
- "How Alex Hormozi grew Gym Launch to $100M"
- "The system 10,000+ freelancers use"
- "Backed by 20 years of research"

### Types of Social Proof

**1. Authority credentials:**
- "15 years in the industry"
- "Former VP at Google"
- "Built and sold 3 companies"

**2. Results achieved:**
- "Grew from 5 to 150 clients"
- "Saved $12,000 per year"
- "6 exits, none VC-backed"

**3. Scale/popularity:**
- "Used by 10,000+ businesses"
- "Trusted by 500+ agencies"
- "1.5 million downloads"

**4. Third-party validation:**
- "Featured in The New York Times"
- "Backed by Harvard research"
- "Recommended by industry leaders"

**5. Expert endorsement:**
- "Naval Ravikant's approach to wealth"
- "What Tim Ferriss says about productivity"
- "Seth Godin's method"

**6. User results:**
- "How @username went from X to Y"
- "The system that worked for [name]"

### When to Include

✅ **Include when:**
- You have relevant credentials/results
- Borrowing authority from respected figure
- Scale adds legitimacy
- Results are impressive and relevant

❌ **Skip when:**
- Would feel like bragging
- Credentials aren't relevant
- No meaningful social proof to share
- Audience doesn't care about authority

### Bragging vs Authority

**Authority (good):**
"10 years building businesses. Here's what worked:"

**Bragging (bad):**
"I'm the best entrepreneur ever. Let me tell you why:"

### Combining Social Proof Types

"How Sara Blakely (founder of Spanx) grew from selling fax machines to building a billion-dollar brand"

This combines:
- Authority (founder)
- Results (billion-dollar)
- Transformation (fax machines → empire)

## Commandment 10: Warning and Caution

### Principle
Create sense of urgency or importance through warning

### Why It Works
- Signals critical information
- Creates FOMO (fear of missing out)
- Implies reader might be at risk
- Urgency compels immediate action

### Checklist Questions
- [ ] Does it suggest reader might be missing something?
- [ ] Is there time sensitivity or urgency?
- [ ] Does it warn of potential pitfall?
- [ ] Would it make someone prioritize reading this?

### Examples

**❌ No urgency:**
- "Tips for marketing"
- "How businesses grow"
- "Information about sales"

**✅ Warning/urgency:**
- "Before you hire an agency, read this"
- "The mistake that costs freelancers $100K+"
- "Warning: This common practice is killing your conversions"

### Ways to Apply

**1. "Before you..." format:**
- "Before you launch, ask these 3 questions"
- "Before you quit your job, read this"

**2. "Warning:" prefix:**
- "Warning: This common pricing mistake costs you $1000s"
- "Warning: Not all automation is created equal"

**3. Time urgency:**
- "Applications close November 10"
- "This is changing fast"
- "Don't wait until [consequence]"

**4. Consequence framing:**
- "What [inaction] is costing you"
- "The hidden cost of [common practice]"

**5. Mistake prevention:**
- "The costly mistake 90% make"
- "Avoid these 3 pitfalls"
- "Don't make the mistake I did"

### Types of Urgency

**1. Time-based:**
"Applications close November 10"

**2. Consequence-based:**
"Every day without this system costs you leads"

**3. Opportunity-based:**
"Spots are filling fast"

**4. Information-based:**
"What you don't know about [topic] is hurting you"

### When to Use

✅ **Use when:**
- There IS genuine urgency
- Consequence of delay is real
- Want to move reader from awareness to action
- Information is time-sensitive

❌ **Don't use when:**
- Creating false urgency
- Content is evergreen/not time-sensitive
- Would feel manipulative
- Audience is already anxious (don't add more pressure)

### Ethical Guidelines

**Real urgency:** "Applications close November 10" ✅
**False urgency:** "Read this NOW or fail forever" ❌

**Helpful warning:** "Before you buy, here's what to know" ✅
**Manipulative:** "If you don't do this, you're a failure" ❌

## Scoring Your Hooks

### How to Score

For each hook variation, count how many commandments apply:

**10/10:** Probably overkill, may feel manipulative
**7-9/10:** Very strong, high engagement likely
**4-6/10:** Good, aim for this range
**2-3/10:** Weak, needs optimization
**0-1/10:** Generic, start over

### Example Scoring

**Hook:** "1.5 million businesses are using this now. Here's the surprising truth about why it works:"

Commandments applied:
1. ✅ Numbers (1.5 million)
2. ❌ Negativity bias
3. ✅ Pattern interrupt ("surprising truth")
4. ❌ Target callout
5. ❌ Problem callout
6. ✅ Confidence ("Here's")
7. ✅ Aesthetics (clean, short)
8. ✅ Benefit (will learn "why it works")
9. ✅ Social proof (scale = 1.5M)
10. ❌ Warning

**Score: 6/10** - Strong hook

### Optimization Strategy

If score is 3 or below, ask:
1. Can I add a specific number?
2. Can I name the target audience?
3. Can I make the benefit clearer?
4. Can I add urgency/warning?

Don't force all 10. Natural application > checklist completion.

Works best with: **Authority + Efficiency** or **Experience + Recommendation**

### When to Use
- You have extensive testing experience
- Want to position as time-saving
- Audience is overwhelmed by options
- You've done the vetting work

### 10 Commandments Applied
✅ Social proof (authority through experience)
✅ Numbers (specific tests)
✅ Useful (saves time)
✅ Specificity (exact recommendations)

## Combining Multiple Formulas

The most powerful headlines often combine elements from 2-3 formulas:

**Example 1:** How-to + Without + Timeframe
- "How to Build a 6-Figure Agency in 90 Days Without Prior Experience"
- Combines: Formula 1 + Formula 7 + specific timeline

# Hook Types - Expanded Guide

This reference expands on the 6 core hook types with detailed formulas, examples, and use cases.

---

## 1. Curiosity Hooks

**Goal:** Create information gap that compels reading

**Techniques:**
- State what and why, leave out how
- State start and end, leave out middle
- Make bold claim, leave out data/proof

**Formulas:**

| Formula | Structure | Example |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Years of Experience | "[X] years in [field]. Here's what [audience] should know:" | "10 years in SEO. Here's what every new digital marketer should know:" |
| Bold Claim Teaser | "[Surprising result]. Here's what happened:" | "I turned 33 today. I weighed in at 337lbs this week. Here's what happened and how I'll lose 140lbs in the next 18 months:" |
| Number Teaser | "[X] [things]. Here's the [best/worst/most important]:" | "I've tested 100+ productivity apps. Here are the only 3 you need:" |

**When to use:**
- Content that reveals process, method, or surprising insight
- You have valuable "how" information to share
- Audience already understands the "what" and wants the "how"

**Examples:**

```
"I spent $50,000 testing Facebook ads. Here's what actually works:"
[Creates gap: what works? Reader must click to find out]

"Most founders fail at fundraising. Here's why:"
[Creates gap: what's the reason? Reader wants to know]

"I hired 47 freelancers on Upwork. These 3 were worth it:"
[Creates gap: which 3? What made them different?]
```

---

## 2. Transformation Hooks

**Goal:** Show compelling before → after journey

**Techniques:**
- Personal transformation stories
- Skill development timelines
- Business/financial progress
- Identity shifts

**Formulas:**

| Formula | Structure | Example |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Numbers Transformation | "[Before number] → [After number]. Here's how:" | "100 followers when I started. Last week, hit 10k. Here's how I did it:" |
| Time-Based Journey | "I [achieved result] in [timeframe]. The key? [Hook]" | "I quit my 9-5 in 6 months. The key? Mastering one high-income skill." |
| Identity Shift | "I used to be [before]. Now I'm [after]. Here's what changed:" | "I used to be a broke freelancer. Now I run a $2M agency. Here's what changed:" |

**When to use:**
- Content showing how to achieve a desirable change
- You have personal story of transformation
- Audience wants similar results
- The "before" state is relatable

**Examples:**

```
"$0 in savings at 25. $100K at 30. Here's my exact strategy:"
[Clear transformation with specific numbers and timeline]

"From burned out employee to thriving solopreneur earning 6 figures.
Here's the pivot:"
[Identity and income transformation combined]

"I couldn't write 500 words a day. Now I write 3,000.
What changed? These 4 habits:"
[Skill transformation with specific numbers]
```

---

## 3. Authority Hooks

**Goal:** Leverage expertise or results to establish credibility

**Techniques:**
- Share years of experience
- Highlight specific achievements
- Reference clients/students with results
- Borrow authority from others (mentions, partnerships)

**Formulas:**

| Formula | Structure | Example |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Experience Declaration | "[X] years in [field]. Here's what [audience] should know:" | "I've built and sold 3 startups. Here are 17 learnings for your entrepreneurial journey:" |
| Client Results | "I [helped client] achieve [result]. Here's how:" | "I coached @TaylinSimmonds to $40,000/month writing tweets. If you want to make $10K/month as a ghostwriter, read this:" |
| Scale Authority | "I've [done thing] [big number] times. Here's what works:" | "I've launched 50+ products. Only these 7 strategies consistently worked:" |

**When to use:**
- You have credentials/results that establish credibility
- Audience values expertise in your field
- Results are specific and impressive
- You can back up claims with proof

**Examples:**

```
"I've written 1,000+ LinkedIn posts. These 10 formats get the most engagement:"
[Volume establishes pattern recognition authority]

"Helped 47 clients land their first $10K month.
Here's the framework they all used:"
[Client results establish coaching authority]

"15 years in corporate marketing. Here's why I ditched it to build my own thing:"
[Insider perspective creates compelling authority]
```

---

## 4. Problem Callout Hooks

**Goal:** Identify pain immediately to capture attention

**Techniques:**
- Name the specific struggle
- Call out the mistake/gap
- Challenge common practice
- Highlight costly errors

**Formulas:**

| Formula | Structure | Example |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Mistake Pattern | "Are you making these [X] [topic] mistakes?" | "98.9% of people are still unaware of ChatGPT's full potential. Here are 10 advanced prompts:" |
| Cost Warning | "The costly mistake [X]% of [audience] make" | "The costly mistake 90% of writers make (and how to avoid it)" |
| Wrong Approach | "Stop doing [common practice]. Do this instead:" | "Stop writing daily. Start writing strategically. Here's the difference:" |

**When to use:**
- Audience has clear pain point they're aware of
- You can identify specific mistake patterns
- Common approach is ineffective
- Readers will recognize themselves in the problem

**Examples:**

```
"Your headlines are costing you readers. Here's why:"
[Direct problem callout for writers]

"Most productivity advice is wrong. Here's what actually works:"
[Challenges common wisdom, implies better solution]

"If you're not doing this, you're leaving money on the table:"
[Cost/consequence framing creates urgency]
```

---

## 5. Target Callout Hooks

**Goal:** Speak directly to specific audience to create relevance

**Techniques:**
- Name the audience explicitly
- Address their specific context
- Use "you" language for direct address
- Reference shared experiences/identity

**Formulas:**

| Formula | Structure | Example |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Direct Address | "[Audience name]... [Action/insight they need]" | "Digital Writers... Stop committing these 4 headline mistakes" |
| Identity + Problem | "If you're [identity], you need to know [this]" | "If you're a solopreneur, you need to know these 7 automation tools" |
| Shared Struggle | "Every [role] deals with [problem]. Here's the solution:" | "Every new parent deals with sleep deprivation. Here's what actually worked:" |

**When to use:**
- Niche content for specific audience segment
- Audience has strong identity/role connection
- Generic hooks won't cut through noise
- You're speaking to a specific community

**Examples:**

```
"Attention Bloggers: Your headlines are costing you readers"
[Direct callout with immediate problem]

"Solopreneurs: Stop feeling guilty about saying no to clients.
Here's a better framework:"
[Identity + problem + solution promise]

"If you're considering going freelance full-time, read this first:"
[Conditional targeting catches people in decision mode]
```

## 6. Bold Claim Hooks

**Goal:** Pattern interrupt with surprising/counterintuitive statement

**Techniques:**
- Make counterintuitive statement
- Challenge conventional wisdom
- Present surprising data
- Take controversial position

**Formulas:**

| Formula | Structure | Example |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Counterintuitive | "[Surprising claim]. Here's why:" | "AI will create millionaires. Here's how you can be part of it:" |
| Forget Everything | "Forget everything you know about [topic]" | "Forget everything you know about headline writing. This changes the game:" |
| Opposite Day | "Don't [expected advice]. Do [opposite] instead:" | "Don't post every day. Post every week. Here's why it works better:" |

**When to use:**
- You have a contrarian or surprising angle
- Conventional wisdom is flawed
- You can back up the bold claim
- Audience is fatigued by standard advice

**Examples:**

```
"The best marketing doesn't happen on social media.
Here's where it happens:"
[Challenges fundamental assumption about marketing]

"I stopped networking. My income doubled. Here's what I did instead:"
[Counterintuitive result creates curiosity]

"More content isn't the answer. Better distribution is.
Here's the proof:"
[Challenges volume-focused conventional wisdom]
```

Often the most powerful hooks combine multiple types:

**Transformation + Authority:**
```

[Shows transformation + establishes authority]
```

**Problem Callout + Bold Claim:**


## Selection Decision Tree

Use this to choose the right hook type:

**Do you have impressive credentials/results?**
→ YES: Consider **Authority** hook

**Is the content about achieving change?**
→ YES: Consider **Transformation** hook

**Do you have a contrarian/surprising angle?**
→ YES: Consider **Bold Claim** hook

**Is there a specific pain point audience recognizes?**
→ YES: Consider **Problem Callout** hook

**Is this for a specific niche audience?**
→ YES: Consider **Target Callout** hook

**Are you revealing a process/method/insight?**
→ YES: Consider **Curiosity** hook

**Still unsure?**
→ Generate 2 options from different types and test both

## Common Mistakes by Hook Type

**Curiosity Hooks:**
❌ Creating gap without payoff (clickbait)
❌ Being too vague ("You won't believe what happened")
✅ Specific tease that you deliver on

**Transformation Hooks:**
❌ Unrelatable "before" state
❌ Unbelievable "after" state
✅ Relatable journey with credible results

**Authority Hooks:**
❌ Humble bragging without value promise
❌ Credentials without relevance
✅ Results that directly relate to audience goal

**Problem Callout Hooks:**
❌ Problem reader doesn't recognize
❌ Pain without solution promise
✅ Specific problem with clear path to fix

**Target Callout Hooks:**
❌ Too broad targeting ("Everyone...")
❌ Exclusionary when you want broad appeal
✅ Specific enough to create relevance

**Bold Claim Hooks:**
❌ Contrarian for shock value only
❌ Can't back up the claim
✅ Surprising but provable statement

# Ideation Process - Detailed Guide

This reference provides extended examples and techniques for Phase 1: Ideation.

## The "Start Questioning" Method - Deep Dive

### How It Works

Ask who/what/where/when/why/how about your topic. Then dig deeper by asking multiple times on the same answer.

**The key:** Don't stop at the first answer. The first answer is usually obvious. The gold is 2-3 levels deeper.

---

### Extended Example 1: Productized Services

**Topic:** Productized services for freelancers

**Round 1: Surface Questions**
- **What?** "What are productized services?" → Fixed-scope packages at set prices
- **Who?** "Who creates them?" → Freelancers, consultants, agencies
- **Where?** "Where do they sell?" → Websites, marketplaces, referrals
- **When?** "When did they become popular?" → Growing rapidly last 5 years
- **Why?** "Why do freelancers choose them?" → Predictable income, easier sales, less scope creep
- **How?** "How do they work?" → Package expertise into repeatable deliverables

**Round 2: Dig Deeper on Promising Angles**

Chose "Why do freelancers choose them?" to explore deeper:

- **Why does predictable income matter?** → Easier to plan and grow
- **Why does that matter?** → Less stress, more focus on quality
- **Why is that important?** → Freelancers who actually ENJOY their work vs constantly chasing clients
- **What's the transformation?** → From anxious hustler to confident business owner

Chose "How do they start?" to explore deeper:

- **How do they start?** → Pick one thing you do repeatedly, package it
- **What do you need?** → Not much - no special tools required
- **Why is that significant?** → Removes the "I'm not ready" barrier
- **What's the insight?** → **"You don't need a fancy funnel to start a productized service"**

**Impact angle discovered:** "You don't need a fancy funnel to start a productized service"

This became the hook angle because it challenges assumption (you need complex systems to sell services) and empowers action (anyone can do this).

---

### Extended Example 2: Corporate Exec Gone Solopreneur

**Topic:** Fortune 500 executive who left to build a one-person business

**Round 1: Surface Questions**
- **What?** "What's the story?" → Executive left to go solo
- **Who?** "Who is she?" → 20+ year corporate leader, knows big business inside-out
- **Why?** "Why did she leave?" → Saw system limitations firsthand
- **How?** "How did it turn out?" → Earns more working less, fully independent
- **What's surprising?** → Person who RAN corporate teams chose NOT to build another one

**Round 2: Dig Deeper**

Chose "Why solo specifically?" to explore:

- **Why not just start another company with a team?** → Wanted to stay in control and keep it simple
- **Why does that matter?** → Less overhead, more creative freedom
- **What's the belief?** → Leverage matters more than headcount
- **What's the proof?** → Out-earns old salary, works 25 hours/week, no employees

Chose "What's surprising about this?" to explore:

- **Why is an insider choosing not to build a team?** → She saw what big teams can't provide
- **What can't they provide?** → True flexibility, creative control, work-life integration
- **What's the transformation for readers?** → From "you need a team to scale" to "leverage beats headcount"

**Angles discovered:**
1. "The Fortune 500 VP who chose to stay solo"
2. "Leverage matters more than headcount"
3. "How a corporate insider built a one-person million-dollar business"

---

### Extended Example 3: Mastery Learning Trap

**Topic:** Mastery-based learning and its potential downsides

**Round 1: Surface Questions**
- **What?** "What is mastery learning?" → Must master topic before moving forward
- **Why?** "Why do educators like it?" → No gaps in knowledge
- **What's the problem?** → Can be too rigid, creates bottlenecks
- **Who does this affect?** → Students who learn differently or at different paces

**Round 2: Dig Deeper**

Chose "What's the actual problem?" to explore:

- **What happens when kid gets stuck?** → Can't move forward until mastery
- **Why is that a problem?** → Might master it LATER, but blocked NOW
- **What's the cost?** → Lose momentum, motivation drops
- **What's the insight?** → Sometimes moving forward maintains motivation better than perfection

Chose "What's the alternative?" to explore:

- **What would work better?** → Strategic incompleteness - know enough to progress
- **Why does that work?** → Maintains momentum, can circle back later
- **What's the metaphor?** → Like video games - you don't 100% every level before advancing
- **What's the transformation?** → From "must master everything" to "progress matters more than perfection"

**Impact angle discovered:** "The Mastery Trap: Why knowing 80% might be better than 100%"

## Headline Elements Extraction

### The 6 Elements Explained

**1. Problem** - What pain/struggle exists?
- Must be specific, not generic
- Reader should immediately recognize it
- Examples: "Freelancers fear feast-or-famine cycles" vs "Business is hard"

**2. Goal** - What result does reader want?
- Concrete outcome, not abstract wish
- Examples: "Confident, independent kids" vs "Better education"

**3. Benefit** - What will their life look like after?
- Paint specific picture of transformed state
- Examples: "Kids who out-earn parents" vs "Successful kids"

**4. Concept** - What idea/principle applies?
- The underlying truth or framework
- Examples: "Initiative matters more than algebra" vs "Learning is important"

**5. Example** - What story/data illustrates this?
- Specific proof point or case study
- Examples: "Executive who built a one-person $1M business" vs "Some people work independently"

**6. Process** - What steps lead to the result?
- The how-to or methodology
- Examples: "Package one skill, price it simply, sell to one audience" vs "Start a business"

### Extraction Practice: Ken Danford Interview

**Content:** Ken Danford runs North Star, a self-directed learning center for teens

**Extracting the 6 elements:**

**Problem:** Teens feel disconnected from their education, going through motions
**Goal:** Engaged, self-motivated learners who own their education
**Benefit:** Teens who discover passions, pursue meaningful projects, thrive in college/careers
**Concept:** Learning is natural when you remove coercion and add mentorship
**Example:** 20+ years of teens who attended North Star, went on to successful college/career paths
**Process:** Teens choose projects, mentors guide, community supports, natural curiosity drives learning

**Element combinations to test:**

| Combination | Headline |
|-------------|----------|
| Problem + Process | "How to turn disconnected teens into self-motivated learners: Remove coercion, add mentorship" |
| Example + Concept | "20 years of self-directed teens prove learning is natural (when you do this)" |
| Goal + Benefit | "Create engaged learners who discover their passions: The North Star model" |
| Concept + Problem | "Learning is natural. Here's what kills it (and how to fix it)" |

## "Become the Reader" Check - Detailed

### The 3 Critical Questions

**1. "What's in it for me?"**

Test: Read your hook and immediately answer: "So what?" from reader's perspective.

❌ **Too vague:** "Learn about productized services"
→ So what? Why does this matter to me?

✅ **Clear benefit:** "Discover why top freelancers switched to productized services (and if it might fit your business)"
→ Aha! This tells me if I should consider this option.

**2. "Is this too abstract?"**

Test: Can you picture what this promises? If not, neither can your reader.

❌ **Too abstract:** "Optimize your client acquisition strategy"
→ What does that even look like?

✅ **Concrete:** "How one freelancer cut sales calls from 10/week to 2 (while doubling revenue)"
→ I can picture this exact scenario and transformation.

**3. "Would I stop scrolling?"**

Test: Honest gut check - in a feed of 100 posts, does this make you pause?

❌ **Boring:** "Important information about pricing strategies"
→ Would scroll past without noticing.

✅ **Scroll-stopping:** "The Fortune 500 VP who left to run a one-person business (and out-earns her old salary)"
→ Wait, what? That's surprising. I'd click.

---

### Example Check: Productized Services Headline

**Draft headline:** "Productized services are growing in popularity"

**"What's in it for me?" check:**
❌ So what? Why should I care?
→ Fails. No clear benefit or relevance to reader.

**Too abstract check:**
❌ What does "growing in popularity" mean? What does that change for me?
→ Fails. Too vague to picture.

**Would I stop scrolling check:**
❌ Boring. Generic trend statement.
→ Fails.

**Revised headline:** "Productized services now power 40% of top agencies. Here's why that number matters for your freelance business."

**"What's in it for me?" check:**
✅ Scale validation might mean this is viable option for me.
→ Passes.

**Too abstract check:**
✅ I can picture: mainstream option, validated at scale, worth considering.
→ Passes.

**Would I stop scrolling check:**
✅ Surprising stat, unexpected comparison, relevance promised.
→ Passes.

## Common Ideation Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Stopping at Surface Level

❌ **Example:**
- Question: "What are productized services?"
- Answer: "Fixed-price packages"
- Hook: "Learn about fixed-price packages"
→ Boring. No depth.

✅ **Better:**
- Question: "What are productized services?"
- Answer: "Fixed-price packages"
- **Dig deeper:** "Why do freelancers choose them?"
- **Dig deeper:** "What makes them different from retainers?"
- **Dig deeper:** "What do clients get that they wouldn't elsewhere?"
- Answer: "Predictability + defined scope combo"
- Hook: "The one thing productized services offer that hourly billing and retainers both miss"
→ Interesting. Specific insight.

### Mistake 2: Generic Transformation

❌ **Generic:**
- Problem: Business isn't working
- Goal: Better business
- Path: Try alternatives

✅ **Specific:**
- Problem: Freelancers hate chasing clients and feel trapped in feast-or-famine cycles
- Goal: Predictable income and feeling in control of the business
- Path: Discover productized services as accessible scalable alternative

### Mistake 3: No Clear "What's In It For Me"

❌ **Unclear benefit:**
"The story of how one freelancer switched to productized services"
→ Interesting story, but what do I get from reading?

✅ **Clear benefit:**
"What one freelancer learned from 3 years of productized services (and 3 mistakes they'd avoid)"
→ I'll learn lessons and avoid mistakes.

# Power Words Library

Words and phrases that add energy, emotion, and urgency to hooks and headlines.

## What Are Power Words?

Power words are emotionally charged words that:
- Trigger psychological responses
- Create urgency or curiosity
- Add energy to flat copy
- Enhance persuasive impact
- Make content more clickable/shareable

**Use power words to:**
- Strengthen weak hooks
- Add emotional punch
- Create pattern interrupts
- Enhance urgency
- Build credibility

**DON'T overuse:** Packing too many power words makes copy feel manipulative or spammy.

---

## The 3 Categories

### Category 1: Buzz Words
Words that create excitement and energy

### Category 2: Emotional Triggers
Words that evoke specific emotions (curiosity, fear, desire)

### Category 3: Powerful Phrases
Multi-word phrases that add impact

---

## CATEGORY 1: Buzz Words

### Energy & Excitement
Hacks, shifts, tips, tricks, tactics, strategies, secrets, formulas, systems, frameworks, blueprints, playbooks, roadmaps, shortcuts

**Examples in use:**
- "7 hacks to 10x your productivity"
- "The formula successful founders use"
- "My exact system for [result]"

---

### Simplicity & Ease
Simple, easy, quick, fast, instant, effortless, painless, straightforward, basic, fundamental, essential, core, tiny, small, minimal

**Examples in use:**
- "A simple way to [solve problem]"
- "The quick guide to [topic]"
- "Essential tools for [audience]"

---

### Intensity & Power
Insanely, incredibly, remarkably, massively, profoundly, dramatically, explosive, powerful, ultimate, complete, definitive, comprehensive, proven

**Examples in use:**
- "The ultimate guide to [topic]"
- "Proven strategies that actually work"
- "Dramatically improve [result]"

---

### Exclusivity & Rarity
Exclusive, limited, rare, scarce, little-known, hidden, secret, insider, private, members-only, invite-only, selective

**Examples in use:**
- "Little-known strategies that [result]"
- "The secret method [experts] use"
- "Exclusive access to [resource]"

---

Proven, tested, verified, validated, backed, certified, authenticated, confirmed, documented, researched, data-driven

## Combining Power Words with Frameworks

### Power Words + Headline Formulas:

**How-to + Emotional Trigger:**
"How to Finally Stop [Pain Point]"
- How-to formula + "finally" (relief trigger)

**List + Buzz Words:**
"7 Little-Known Secrets to [Result]"
- List formula + "little-known" (exclusivity) + "secrets" (curiosity)

**Question + Fear Trigger:**
"Are You Making These Costly Mistakes?"
- Question formula + "costly" (fear/loss aversion)

---

### Power Words + Thread Hooks:

**Authority Hook + Powerful Phrase:**
"10 years building remote teams. Here's the truth nobody tells you:"
- Authority + "truth nobody tells you" phrase

**Transformation Hook + Power Words:**
"From burned out employee to thriving solopreneur. Here's the unconventional path I took:"
- Transformation + "thriving" (success) + "unconventional" (curiosity)

---

## Strategic Use Guidelines

### When to Use Power Words:

✅ **Weak hooks that need energy**
- Before: "Tips for better learning"
- After: "7 little-known strategies that skyrocket learning"

✅ **Creating urgency**
- Before: "Things to know about freelancing"
- After: "Critical mistakes to avoid before going freelance"

✅ **Adding credibility**
- Before: "My approach to consulting"
- After: "The proven system I used to grow 3 consulting businesses"

✅ **Breaking through noise**
- Before: "Why small agencies are good"
- After: "The surprising truth about why small agencies outperform"

---

### When NOT to Use Power Words:

❌ **When clarity is most important**
- Instructions, tutorials, technical content

❌ **Packing multiple power words into one hook**
- "The ultimate proven revolutionary secret system" (too much)

❌ **When it creates false expectations**
- Don't promise "effortless" if it requires work

❌ **Professional/serious contexts where they feel manipulative**
- Academic writing, formal communications

---

## Power Word Density Guidelines

**Subject lines / Short hooks (5-10 words):**
- 1-2 power words maximum
- Example: "The proven system for [result]"

**Thread hooks (15-25 words):**
- 2-3 power words maximum
- Example: "10 years in [field]. Here's the counterintuitive truth nobody tells you:"

**Headline (8-15 words):**
- 1-3 power words depending on length
- Example: "The Ultimate Guide to Building a Thriving Consulting Practice"

**Rule of thumb:** More than 3 power words in a single sentence feels spammy.

---

## Testing Power Words

**A/B Test Different Power Word Categories:**

Test 1: Authority words
- "The proven method for [result]"
- "The tested system for [result]"

Test 2: Emotional triggers
- "The surprising truth about [topic]"
- "The weird secret to [topic]"

Test 3: Simplicity words
- "The simple guide to [topic]"
- "The quick method for [topic]"

**Track which categories resonate with your audience**

---

## Industry-Specific Power Word Guidelines

When applying power words to your niche, consider:

**B2B/Professional Services:**
- Strong performers: Proven, efficient, scalable, automated, ROI-driven
- Avoid: Hyped words like "revolutionary," "explosive" (too informal)

**Creative/Personal Brands:**
- Strong performers: Authentic, unconventional, fresh, bold, personal
- Avoid: Corporate jargon, overly salesy language

**Health/Wellness:**
- Strong performers: Natural, holistic, gentle, sustainable, effective
- Avoid: "Miracle," "instant," "cure" (overpromising, potentially problematic)

**Finance/Business:**
- Strong performers: Profitable, strategic, smart, disciplined, compounding
- Avoid: "Guaranteed," "risk-free" (legal/ethical issues)

**Education/Coaching:**
- Strong performers: Transformative, practical, actionable, proven, hands-on
- Avoid: Effortless, instant (learning takes work, don't oversell)

---

## Quality Checklist

Before using a power word, ask:

- [ ] **Does it add meaning or just hype?**
- [ ] **Is it accurate to the content?**
- [ ] **Does it fit the brand voice?**
- [ ] **Would I roll my eyes reading this?**
- [ ] **Does it help or manipulate?**

If 4/5 yes, the power word is appropriate.

# Sticky Sentence Techniques

Literary devices that make sentences memorable, quotable, and "sticky" in readers' minds.

## What Makes a Sentence Sticky?

A sticky sentence is:
- **Memorable** - Easy to recall hours or days later
- **Quotable** - People want to share it
- **Simple** - Not complex or convoluted
- **Punchy** - Has rhythm or impact
- **Universal** - Applies beyond one specific context

**Use sticky sentences for:**
- Thread hooks
- Newsletter subject lines
- Key insights within content
- Brand taglines
- Quotable moments in long-form

---

## The 5 Core Techniques

### 1. Alliteration
### 2. Symmetry
### 3. Contrast
### 4. Rhyme
### 5. Rhythm

---

## Technique 1: Alliteration

### Definition
Using words that start with the same sound in close succession

### Why It Works
- Creates pleasing sound pattern
- Easier to remember (brain likes patterns)
- Adds emphasis and rhythm
- Feels intentional and crafted

### Examples

- "Specificity is the secret." - @ItsKieranDrew
- "The best jobs are neither decreed nor degreed." - Naval Ravikant
- "Simplicity is the ultimate sophistication." - Steve Jobs
- "Permission beats perfectionism."
- "Make writing the medium. Make exploration the habit."
- "Progress prefers patience."
- "Clarity creates confidence."
- "Structure supports, it doesn't suffocate."
- "Mastery matters more than motion."

### Formula
```
[S-word] is the [S-word]
[P-word] [P-word] [P-word]
[Word] beats [similar-sound-word]
```

### How to Create

**Step 1:** Identify your core message
Example: "Being specific helps"

**Step 2:** Find synonyms starting with same letter
- Specific → specificity
- Helps → secret, solution, superpower

**Step 3:** Combine for impact
Result: "Specificity is the secret"

**Practice Exercise:**
Take these concepts and make them alliterative:
- "Freedom is important" → "Freedom over formality"
- "Community matters" → "Community creates connection"
- "Questions help thinking" → "Questions quicken curiosity"

---

## Technique 2: Symmetry

### Definition
Creating balance in sentence structure through parallel phrases

### Why It Works
- Creates satisfying rhythm
- Emphasizes contrast or connection
- Easier to remember (pattern matching)
- Feels complete and authoritative

### Examples

- "Read for awareness. Write for understanding."
- "It's not 10,000 hours. It's 10,000 iterations." - Naval Ravikant
- "Make writing the medium. Make exploration the habit."
- "A lack of negatives outweighs an abundance of positives."
- "Design for function. Build for beauty."
- "Input shapes output. Environment shapes behavior."
- "Amateurs focus on tactics. Professionals focus on systems."
- "Busy people count hours. Effective people count outcomes."
- "Credentials signal past learning. Skills signal current capability."

### Formula

**Pattern A: [Action 1] for [outcome]. [Action 2] for [outcome].**
- "Read for awareness. Write for understanding."
- "Learn for knowledge. Create for impact."

**Pattern B: It's not [X]. It's [Y].**
- "It's not 10,000 hours. It's 10,000 iterations."
- "It's not about talent. It's about reps."

**Pattern C: [Noun 1] [verb] [object]. [Noun 2] [verb] [object].**
- "Amateurs focus on tactics. Professionals focus on systems."
- "Managers deliver tasks. Leaders deliver vision."

### How to Create

**Step 1:** Identify two related concepts
Example: "Input vs Output"

**Step 2:** Find parallel structures
- Both can be "shaped by" something
- Input shapes → behavior, skills, mindset
- Output shapes → results, reputation, growth

**Step 3:** Create balance
Result: "Input shapes output. Environment shapes behavior."

**Practice Exercise:**
Create symmetrical sentences for:
- Short-term vs long-term thinking
- Effort vs results
- Consumption vs creation

---

## Technique 3: Contrast

### Definition
Juxtaposing opposing ideas to create impact

### Why It Works
- Highlights extremes
- Forces binary choice
- Creates tension and resolution
- Memorable because of stark difference

### Examples

- "To be everywhere is to be nowhere." - Letters from a Stoic
- "Your simple guide to massive results."
- "Be clear, not clever. Concise, not complex." - @ItsKieranDrew
- "Strong opinions, loosely held."
- "Think big. Start small."
- "Small team. Big impact."
- "Less effort, more leverage."
- "Simple system. Profound results."
- "Short sprints. Deep work."
- "Fewer options, better decisions."
- "No meetings, real progress."

### Formula

**Pattern A: [Adjective 1], not [Adjective 2]**
- "Clear, not clever"
- "Simple, not simplistic"
- "Bold, not reckless"

**Pattern B: [Small thing]. [Big impact].**
- "Small team. Big impact."
- "One decision. Everything changes."

**Pattern C: [A] to [extreme]. [B] to [opposite extreme].**
- "To be everywhere is to be nowhere."
- "To do everything is to do nothing."

### How to Create

**Step 1:** Identify your point
Example: "Small teams can achieve a lot"

**Step 2:** Find opposing descriptors
- Small ↔ Big
- Few ↔ Many
- Simple ↔ Complex

**Step 3:** Create tension
Result: "Small team. Big impact."

**Practice Exercise:**
Create contrasts for:
- Flexibility vs rigidity
- Slow and steady vs fast and scattered
- Old methods vs new approaches

---

## Technique 4: Rhyme

### Definition
Using words with similar ending sounds

### Why It Works
- Extremely memorable
- Feels playful and intentional
- Creates rhythm
- Easy to recall

### Examples

- "Tell a story or lose your glory."
- "Plan your work and work your plan."
- "You can't soar with eagles if you hang with turkeys."
- "If it doesn't make dollars, it doesn't make sense."
- "Clarity brings charity." (people help what they understand)
- "Build community, create unity."
- "Flexibility isn't inability."

### Formula

**Pattern A: [Action] or [rhyming consequence]**
- "Tell a story or lose your glory"
- "Show the proof or lose the truth"

**Pattern B: [Concept], [rhyming concept]**
- "Community creates unity"
- "Consistency builds persistency"

### How to Create

**Step 1:** Identify core message
Example: "Community is important"

**Step 2:** Find rhyming words related to message
- Community → unity, opportunity
- Important → alignment, excitement

**Step 3:** Craft sentence
Result: "Build community, create unity."

**Warning:** Easy to sound cheesy. Use sparingly and ensure it adds value, not just rhymes.

**Practice Exercise:**
Create rhymes for:
- Consistency leads to success
- Taking action matters
- Building trust

---

## Technique 5: Rhythm

### Definition
Creating a pleasing cadence in your sentence through word choice and structure

### Why It Works
- Easier to say aloud (more shareable)
- Creates flow and momentum
- Feels "right" when you read it
- Stands out from normal prose

### Examples

- "When you can't wait to share it, they can't help but read it."
- "Make your sentences sing."
- "The more you learn, the more you earn."
- "Ship fast. Learn faster."
- "Start small. Stay consistent. Scale later."
- "Less talking, more doing. Less planning, more shipping."
- "Small bets. Quick wins. Real momentum."
- "Find the pain. Build the solution. Own the market."
- "Question everything. Test constantly. Iterate forever."

### Formula

**Pattern A: Short. Punchy. Fragments.**
- "Small bets. Quick wins. Real momentum."
- "No fluff. No filler. Just value."

**Pattern B: [The more X, the more Y]**
- "The more you learn, the more you earn."
- "The less you do, the more you accomplish."

**Pattern C: [Action A]. [Action B]. [Result].**
- "Find the pain. Build the solution. Own the market."
- "Set the goal. Do the work. Trust the process."

### How to Create

**Step 1:** List your key points
Example:
- Identify the problem
- Create the solution
- Capture the market

**Step 2:** Use short, similar-length phrases
- Find → Build → Own (parallel verbs)
- The pain → the solution → the market (similar structure)

**Step 3:** Test it aloud
Result: "Find the pain. Build the solution. Own the market."

**Practice Exercise:**
Create rhythmic sentences for:
- Your morning routine (list 3 elements)
- How to build an audience
- Why simplicity wins

---

## Combining Techniques

The most powerful sticky sentences often combine 2-3 techniques:

### Alliteration + Symmetry
"Anger prepares us to fight. Fear prepares us to flee."
- Symmetry: Parallel structure (X prepares us to Y)
- Alliteration: Fight/flee

**More examples:**
"Consistency creates competence. Persistence produces excellence."
- Symmetry: Parallel structure
- Alliteration: Creates/competence, Produces/persistence

---

### Symmetry + Contrast
"Amateurs focus on tactics. Professionals focus on systems."
- Symmetry: Parallel structure
- Contrast: Tactics vs systems (short-term vs long-term)

"Consume for entertainment. Create for impact."
- Symmetry: [Action] for [outcome]
- Contrast: Consume vs create (passive vs active)

---

### Alliteration + Rhythm
"Ship fast. Fail forward. Fix forever."
- Alliteration: Fast/Fail/Fix/Forward/Forever
- Rhythm: Short, punchy cadence

**More examples:**
"Progress prefers patience. Purpose powers persistence."
- Alliteration: Progress/prefers/patience, Purpose/powers/persistence
- Rhythm: Short parallel phrases

---

### Contrast + Rhythm
"Think big. Start small. Ship fast."
- Contrast: Big/small, slow/fast
- Rhythm: Three short commands

**More examples:**
"Dream wide. Start narrow. Go deep."
- Contrast: Wide/narrow, surface/deep
- Rhythm: Three parallel commands

---

### All 5 Combined (Rare but Powerful)
"Read to learn. Write to think. Teach to master."
- Alliteration: Learn/think (soft), master (different but rhythmic)
- Symmetry: [Action] to [outcome] (3x parallel)
- Contrast: Read (input) vs write (output) vs teach (share)
- Rhyme: Think/master (internal rhyme)
- Rhythm: Three short phrases, escalating in syllables

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

❌ **Forcing it** - If it doesn't flow naturally, it won't sound good
❌ **Sacrificing clarity for style** - Sticky means nothing if the meaning is unclear
❌ **Overusing rhyme** - Can sound childish or forced
❌ **Being too clever** - Remember: "Be clear, not clever"
❌ **Using all techniques at once** - Usually 1-2 is most powerful

## Quality Checklist for Sticky Sentences

Test your sentence:

- [ ] **Can I remember it 5 minutes later?** (Memory test)
- [ ] **Would I quote this to a friend?** (Quotability test)
- [ ] **Does it sound good read aloud?** (Rhythm test)
- [ ] **Is the meaning immediately clear?** (Clarity test)
- [ ] **Does it apply beyond this specific context?** (Universal test)

If 4/5 pass, you have a sticky sentence.

## Platform-Specific Adaptations

### For X/Twitter:
- Keep sentences short and punchy
- Use emojis sparingly (🧵 for threads is standard)
- First tweet must hook in <280 characters
- Test both long-form first tweet vs short hook

### For LinkedIn:
- Can be longer and more detailed
- Professional tone (but still conversational)
- First 2 lines show in feed (hook must work there)
- Use line breaks for readability
- Hashtags less important (3-5 max)

### For Facebook:
- More casual, personal tone
- Question-based hooks perform well
- Community/relationship focus
- NO hashtags (Facebook doesn't reward them)
- Works well with visual (post image question)
