# Social Content Creator

Transform any source material into high-performing social media posts using the **framework fitting method**: extract standalone concepts, match to proven templates, generate volume, select best, execute, and proliferate winners.

## Purpose

Content creation isn't about constantly coming up with new ideas. It's about turning 1 idea into 1000 variations using proven frameworks.

**Core Philosophy:** Good content follows proven formats. The skill is in **framework fitting** - matching concepts to the delivery mechanisms that amplify them best.

## When to Use This Skill

- Repurposing newsletter content into social posts
- Transforming long-form articles into multi-platform campaigns
- Creating LinkedIn posts from any concept or story
- Generating multiple post options quickly
- Multiplying high-performing posts using SCAMPER or desire reframing

**Not for:** Short-form video captions (use `video-caption-creation`), podcast clip selection (separate workflow)

---

## The Framework Fitting Method

### Core Principle
Good content follows proven formats/frameworks/templates. The skill is in **framework fitting** - matching concepts to the delivery mechanisms that amplify them best.

### Critical Rule: Avoid First-Match Bias
**Do NOT default to the first template that seems to fit.**

> "Analyze, Then Select" - Treat template selection as a distinct step. Consider ALL applicable frameworks before selecting.

The best framework might not be the obvious one.

---

## The 4-Phase Workflow

### Phase 1: Extract Standalone Concepts

**Goal:** Identify the most shareable "standalone" concepts from source material

#### Step 1: Read Source Material Thoroughly
- Full newsletter, article, or podcast transcript
- Look for bundles of ideas within their context
- Self-contained insights that can live independently

#### Step 2: Extract Concepts
For each standalone concept, capture:
- **Core Insight** (1-2 sentences)
- **Context Needed** (what background info is required?)
- **Emotion/Value** (what does this unlock for the reader?)
- **Human Desire(s)** (which of the 8 desires does this address?)
- **Potential Angles** (initial framework ideas)

**How Many Concepts to Extract:**
- Newsletter segment: 1-3 concepts per segment
- Full newsletter: 5-10 concepts total
- Long-form article: 5-15 concepts
- Podcast episode: 3-8 concepts

#### Step 3: Create Concepts Document

```markdown
# [Source] - Extracted Concepts

## Concept 1: [Title]
**Core Insight:** [1-2 sentences]
**Context:** [What background is needed]
**Emotion/Value:** [What this unlocks for reader]
**Human Desires:** [Which of the 8]
**Potential Angles:** [Framework ideas]

---

## Concept 2: [Title]
[Repeat structure...]
```

### The 8 Human Desires

Reference these when extracting concepts:

1. **Safety of Tribe** - Belonging, community, fitting in
2. **Survival & Success** - Progress, achievement, security
3. **Life Enjoyment** - Pleasure, experiences, freedom
4. **Social Acceptance** - Status, respect, recognition
5. **Sexual Companionship** - Connection, attraction (often not applicable)
6. **Comfort & Clarity** - Simplicity, ease, certainty
7. **Freedom From Fear** - Safety, protection, security
8. **Perceived Status** - Respect, authority, expertise

---

### Phase 2: Framework Matching (THE CRITICAL PHASE)

**Goal:** Match extracted concepts to best-fit frameworks using volume generation → selection

#### The Framework Matching Process

**For each concept:**

1. **Brainstorm First:** How COULD this concept be presented?
   - Think beyond the obvious
   - Consider different platforms
   - Imagine different emotional angles

2. **Review ALL Applicable Frameworks:**
   - LinkedIn templates (86+ options)
   - Text post structures (100+ options)
   - Don't stop at first match
   - Consider at least 5-10 frameworks minimum

3. **Test the Fit:**
   - Does concept naturally fit framework structure?
   - What would make this concept fit BETTER in another framework?
   - Which framework amplifies the core emotion/value most?

4. **Generate Volume:**
   - Quick social plan: 2-4 framework matches per concept
   - High-volume campaign: 10-20 framework matches per concept

5. **Select Best:**
   - Which frameworks amplify the concept most?
   - Which platforms matter most?
   - Which formats are most efficient to execute?

#### Framework Categories

**LinkedIn Templates** (see `references/linkedin-frameworks.md`):
- 86+ templates from LinkedIn swipe files
- 50+ templates from proven creators
- Best for: Thought leadership, educational content, stories

**Text Post Structures** (see `references/post-structures.md`):
- 100+ proven tweet/post frameworks
- Best for: X/Twitter, Facebook, Instagram captions
- Categories: Comparisons, Lists, Challenges, Observations, Analogies

#### Document Framework Matches

```markdown
## Concept: "[Title]"

### Framework Matches Generated (10 total):

**1. Pattern Recognition Template**
- **Fit Assessment:** ✅ STRONG
- **Why it fits:** [Explanation]
- **Structure:** "I've noticed a pattern: [Common Belief] isn't what leads to [Success]... It's [Core Insight]"
- **Platform:** LinkedIn
- **Execution Preview:** "[Draft of how it would read]"

**2. Contrarian Truth Template**
- **Fit Assessment:** ✅ STRONG / ⚠️ MODERATE / ❌ WEAK
- **Why it fits:** [Explanation]
[Continue...]

### SELECTED FOR EXECUTION (Top 3):
1. [Framework] - [Rationale]
2. [Framework] - [Rationale]
3. [Framework] - [Rationale]
```

**Quality Check:**
- [ ] Generated at least 5-10 framework options per concept
- [ ] Assessed fit strength (STRONG/MODERATE/WEAK)
- [ ] Explained WHY concept fits each framework
- [ ] Selected best 2-4 with clear rationale
- [ ] Considered platform appropriateness
- [ ] Avoided first-match bias

### Phase 3: Execute Selected Frameworks

**Goal:** Create polished, platform-optimized posts from selected frameworks

#### Determine Output Scope

- **Quick Social Plan** (1-3 posts): Best framework match per concept
- **Multi-Platform Campaign** (5-10 posts): Multiple frameworks across concepts
- **High-Volume Repurposing** (10-20 posts): 2-3 variations per framework

#### Execute Each Framework

1. **Load Framework Structure** - Reference the specific template
2. **Fill with Concept Details** - Insert insight, context, examples
3. **Apply Voice** - Match brand or individual voice style
4. **Platform-Specific Optimization** - Apply rules below
5. **Generate Variations** - Create 1-3 variations of same framework

#### Platform-Specific Guidelines

**LinkedIn:**
- **Length:** Long-form value-dense posts outperform (200-500 words)
- **Links:** Always in comments (not main post)
- **Hashtags:** 3-5 maximum, minimal
- **Voice:** Professional yet conversational, thought leadership
- **Hook:** First 2 lines critical (shows before "see more")
- **Structure:** Hook → Body → Insight → Question or CTA
- **Tagging:** Tag mentioned people/orgs (increases reach)

**X (Twitter):**
- **Length:** 70-100 characters optimal (despite 280 limit)
- **Hashtags:** 1-2 maximum
- **Voice:** Witty, concise, to-the-point
- **Threads:** Use for longer concepts
- **Current strategy:** Reply game important for growth

**Facebook:**
- **Length:** 40-140 characters optimal
- **Links:** NO external links in main post (kills reach)
- **Hashtags:** NO hashtags (Facebook doesn't reward them)
- **Voice:** Informal, playful, personable
- **Format:** Question posts with images perform best

**Instagram:**
- **Length:** 30-125 characters for feed captions
- **Hashtags:** 5-10 relevant hashtags
- **Voice:** Friendly, authentic, micro-story format
- **Formats:** Reels outperform static, carousels for educational

### Phase 4: Proliferate Winners (OPTIONAL)

**Goal:** Multiply high-performing posts using SCAMPER, Human Desires, and Vision reframing

**When to use:**
- Monthly: Study top performers and multiply them
- Pre-posting: Generate many options, select best
- Content drought: Multiply existing winners

#### Method 1: SCAMPER Variations

Generate 7 variations from one post (one per letter):

**S - Substitute**
- Replace main idea with adjacent concept
- Swap the method for accomplishing benefit
- Change examples while keeping structure

**C - Combine**
- Merge with personal experience or story
- Combine two concepts for more power
- Add reply to someone else's post as your own content

**A - Adapt**
- Expand one-liner into full post or thread
- Add "missing piece" not in original
- Adapt to different platform

**M - Modify**
- Magnify specific pain points
- Use same idea in different format
- Make more punchy by removing words

**P - Purpose** (Put to other use)
- Angle for different audience segment
- Different benefit/pain point this solves

**E - Eliminate**
- Remove words to make punchier
- Simplify complex explanation
- Cut nuance and uncertainty

**R - Reverse**
- Flip the idea completely
- "With X you get Y" → "Without X you stay stuck with Z"
- Positive framing → negative (or vice versa)

**SCAMPER Example:**

Original:
> "The most successful founders don't start with funding. They start with customers."

**S - Substitute:**
> "The most successful founders don't start with a perfect product. They start with a painful problem."

**C - Combine:**
> "I spent years thinking I needed funding to start. Then I talked to 3 founders who bootstrapped to $10M. The most successful don't start with funding. They start with customers."

**R - Reverse:**
> "Without customers, your funding means nothing. Ask any startup that raised $50M and still failed."

#### Method 2: Human Desires Reframing

Spin concept through 8 desire lenses:

**Example concept:** "Customers over funding"

**Safety of Tribe:**
> "You don't need VCs to validate your idea. You need customers who get it. Build your tribe first."

**Survival & Success:**
> "The path to success isn't through investors. Every successful founder knows: customers first, funding later."

**Comfort & Clarity:**
> "Starting a company feels overwhelming. Here's what brings clarity: successful founders don't start with funding. They start with customers. Everything else follows."

**Freedom From Fear:**
> "The fear of 'not having enough runway' stops more companies than lack of funding. Get customers first. The fear disappears."

#### Proliferation Output

Create file: `[Post Title]_Proliferations.md`

Include:
- 7 SCAMPER variations
- 6-8 Human Desire reframes
- **Total: 13-15 new variations from 1 original**

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

### Content Issues
❌ **First-Match Bias** - Picking first framework that fits
❌ **Generic Posts** - Not using any framework
❌ **Too Many Concepts** - Multiple ideas in one post
❌ **Missing Context** - Concept doesn't stand alone

### Framework Issues
❌ **Framework Drift** - Abandoning structure mid-post
❌ **Mismatched Fit** - Forcing concept into wrong framework
❌ **No Volume** - Creating only 1 option instead of 5-10+

### Platform Issues
❌ **Wrong Link Placement** - External links in Facebook/LinkedIn main posts
❌ **Hashtag Overload** - Too many for platform
❌ **Voice Mismatch** - Too formal for Facebook, too casual for LinkedIn

### Process Issues
❌ **Skipping Concept Extraction** - Writing without identifying standalone concepts
❌ **Not Documenting Framework** - Can't reference what worked later

# LinkedIn Content Frameworks

## 1. The "Agree or Disagree" Post

### Description

This strategy works by asking people to participate with a yes/no response while layering it with a personal story that adds emotional depth and relatability.

### Example

```
A lousy job with a good boss is better than a good job with a lousy boss.

Agree?

I was talking to a friend this weekend and they told me about this terrible experience they had at work.

They said to me, "My boss made a mistake on a project we were working on, and instead of owning his mistake, he said it was my fault. I sat through that meeting feeling shocked and disappointed. When we left the meeting, he told me, 'You are a good sport.' I knew I needed to start planning my exit strategy. I love my job, but I can't trust my boss."

It's demotivating being under a bad manager. I've had some bad bosses and it really makes a huge difference on "whom you work with" vs "what you work on" each day. I think a lousy job with a good boss and great co-workers is better than a good job with a lousy boss or co-workers who don't have your back.

I'm curious... what do you think...? Comment below.

```

### Template

```
{{Make a statement}}

Agree or disagree...? Leave it in the comments.

I was talking to a friend this weekend and they told me about this terrible experience they had at work.

They said to me, "{{share the terrible experience}}"

Here's what I've come to learn over the years...

{{insight and a good perspective on life… OR … you can also insert a quote if you can't come up with something}}

I'm curious...what do you think...?

Comment below.

```

### How It Works

- Opens with a controversial or thought-provoking statement that demands a position
- Invites engagement with a simple agree/disagree prompt
- Shares a relatable personal story that illustrates the statement
- Provides your perspective or insight on the issue
- Ends with an explicit call to action for comments

## 2. The "Before and After" Post

### Description

This strategy showcases transformation and results, which is inherently engaging. People love to see evidence of improvement and positive change, especially when it's visual.

### Example

```
[Image showing Before/After transformation]

What does your LinkedIn profile photo say about you?

First impressions matter.

```

### Template

```
[Image showing Before/After transformation]

{{Brief caption explaining the transformation}}

{{Insight about why this matters}}

```

### How It Works

- Uses before/after visuals to demonstrate transformation

# Platform-Specific Guidelines

## LinkedIn

**Length:** Long-form value-dense posts outperform (200-500 words)

**Links:** Always in comments (not main post) - algorithm penalizes external links in main text

**Hashtags:** 3-5 maximum, minimal usage

**Voice:** Professional yet conversational, thought leadership tone

**Hook:** First 2 lines critical - shows in feed before "see more" button

**Structure:**
- Hook (2 lines max)
- Body (story, data, insights)
- Core insight or takeaway
- Question or CTA

**Tagging:** Tag mentioned people/organizations - increases reach and enables nearbound amplification

**Best Performing Formats:**
- Personal stories with business lessons
- Contrarian insights with data
- Pattern recognition posts
- "I've noticed..." observations
- Long-form educational threads

---

## X (Twitter)

**Length:** 70-100 characters optimal (despite 280 limit)

**Hashtags:** NONE

**Voice:** Witty, concise, to-the-point

**Threads:** Use for longer concepts - link to thread in main tweet

**Current Strategy:** Reply game until 500-1000 followers (posting has low ROI currently)

**Thread Best Practices:**
- Hook in first tweet
- 3-7 tweets ideal length
- One idea per tweet
- Visual pattern (bullets, numbers, breaks)
- CTA or question in final tweet

**Best Performing Formats:**
- Hot takes (contrarian but obvious)
- Quick tips (numbered lists)
- Before/After comparisons
- Quote tweets with added perspective

---

## Facebook

**Length:** 40-140 characters optimal

**Links:** NO external links in main post (kills organic reach)
- Share link in first comment instead
- Or use Stories with link stickers

**Hashtags:** NO hashtags (Facebook doesn't reward them, looks spammy)

**Voice:** Informal, playful, personable

**Format:** Question posts with background images perform best

**Content Mix:** 80% category content, 20% program/brand promotion

**Best Performing Formats:**
- Question posts (genuine curiosity, not rhetorical)
- Community conversation starters
- "Tag someone who..." posts
- Personal stories (relatable moments)
- Behind-the-scenes content

**Image Strategy:**
- Use branded background graphics for text posts
- Family/learning photos for stories
- Simple, clean designs (not cluttered)

---

## Instagram

**Feed Posts:**
- **Length:** 30-125 characters for captions
- **Hashtags:** 5-10 relevant hashtags
- **Voice:** Friendly, authentic, micro-story format
- **Best format:** Carousel posts for educational content

**Reels:**
- **Performance:** Outperform static posts for reach/new followers
- **Length:** 15-90 seconds optimal
- **Hook:** First 3 seconds critical
- **Captions:** Use video-caption-creation skill

**Stories:**
- **Performance:** High engagement with existing audience
- **Interaction:** Use stickers, polls, questions
- **Links:** Available with link sticker

**Audience Position:** Further up funnel (problem aware, not solution aware)

**Best Performing Formats:**
- Reels with on-screen text hooks
- Carousel posts (5-10 slides, educational)
- Quote graphics over branded templates
- Behind-the-scenes stories
- User-generated content reposts

**Carousel Structure:**
- Slide 1: Hook (stop the scroll)
- Slides 2-8: Value (tips, insights, steps)
- Final slide: CTA or summary

---

## TikTok

**Length:** 15-60 seconds optimal (can go up to 10 minutes)

**Hashtags:** 3-5 relevant hashtags

**Voice:** Casual, trendy, educational entertainment

**Hook:** First 3 seconds determine everything

**Best Performing Formats:**
- Educational content that feels entertaining
- "Here's what nobody tells you about..."
- Quick tips (3-5 items)
- Story-based teaching
- Trend participation with education twist

**On-Screen Text:** Use video-caption-creation skill

**Algorithm:** Prioritizes watch time and completion rate
- Keep concise

- Deliver value fast
- End with question or CTA

# Effective Tweet and Post Structures

This document contains proven social media post templates and structures that can be used to create engaging content across platforms like Twitter, LinkedIn, and other social media channels.

## Post Structure Templates

### 2x3 Comparison
**Instructions:** Create a visual pattern interrupt. Break apart information for easy consumption.

**Example:**
```
Broke:     Working a 9-5
Wealthy:   Running a business  
Rich:      Investing in assets

Employee:  Trading time for money
Business:  Building systems that make money
Investor:  Making money work for you
```

### 80/20 Rule
**Instructions:** Present a principle based on the Pareto principle. Offer a concise tip for implementation.

**Example:**
```
80% of your day should be filled with doing things you enjoy, minimum.

80% listening
20% talking

Avoid unnecessary chatter that may leave you with regret

Think deeply, then respond
```

### Ability-Outcome Link
**Format:** "Your ability to _ determines your _ level."

**Example:** "Your ability to focus determines your success level."

### Achievement Steps
**Instructions:** List steps to achieve a specific goal.
**Format:** How to (achieve something)?...

**Example:**
```
How do you become an authority? 
1. Build in public 
2. Talk about the same thing for a long time 
3. Demonstrate competence 
4. Share industry insights 
5. Share takes/opinions 
6. Network with other authorities. 

Do all of the above for a long time. It can't be rushed.
```

### Action vs. Words
**Format:** "_ speaks much louder than anything you'll ever say."

**Example:** "Your work ethic speaks much louder than anything you'll ever say."

### Adapted Proverb Hook
**Instructions:** Take a familiar saying and adapt it to a new context

**Example:** "When the reader is ready, the book appears."

### Analogies
**Instructions:** Use a vivid comparison to illustrate a point. Often employs humor or shock value for effect.

**Example:** "Telling everyone your goals is a form of mental masturbation."

### Authority Building Plan
**Instructions:** Share strategies to build authority from scratch.
**Format:** How I plan to build authority as a 'nobody':...

**Example:**
```
How I plan to build authority as a 'nobody': 
- (something) 
- (something) 
- (something) 
- (something) 
- (something). 

(Question for the reader to engage)
```

### Behavior Dichotomy
**Instructions:** Compare the behaviors of two different types of people, showing how they act oppositely.
**Format:** "_ people do 'x'. _ people do '-x'"

**Example:** "Successful people take risks. Unsuccessful people avoid them."

### Benefits List
**Instructions:** State an action or decision. List the benefits or outcomes.

**Example:**
```
Go on more walks if you want to:

• Clear your mind
• Stay lean with ease  
• Become more energetic
• Generate profitable ideas
• Exchange screens for sunlight
• Find direction by removing distractions

Walks are the lowest friction way to solve most of the problems in your life.
```

### Binary Framing Hook
**Instructions:** Frame a complex issue as a choice between two options

**Example:** "Most of the culture wars are a struggle between what is best for the children and what is best for the childless."

### Bragging (humble or blunt)
**Instructions:** List personal habits or achievements. Conclude with a statement that adds context or humor.

**Example:**
```
I take hot showers.
I play video games.
I smoke weed sometimes.
I'm still a millionaire.

I know, crazy concept. 🤯
```

### Cause and Effect
**Format:** "_ today usually lead to _ tomorrow"

**Example:** "Small habits today usually lead to big results tomorrow"

### Cause and Effect Hook
**Instructions:** Describe a simple cause-and-effect relationship

**Example:** "When you can't wait to share it, they can't help but read it."

### Challenging Common Beliefs
**Instructions:** Present a counterintuitive idea. Briefly explain the reasoning behind it.

**Example:** "It's mentally healthy to not be up-to-date about everything all the time."

### Choice Emphasis
**Instructions:** Pick a positive quality or state that people often mistake as an outcome, but is actually a decision.
**Format:** "_ is a choice, not a result."

**Example:** "Happiness is a choice, not a result."

### Commentary
**Instructions:** 
• Start with a common saying or belief in quotes
• Provide your unique perspective or counterpoint  
• Explain your reasoning briefly

**Example:**
```
"I don't have time to exercise"

Translation: I don't prioritize my health enough to make time for it.

Everyone has the same 24 hours. It's about choices, not time.
```

### Commentary on Popular Ideas
**Instructions:** Analyze common concepts from a unique perspective.

**Example:** "If you don't have a purpose, everything becomes your problem. You don't have a way to filter important information that helps you achieve your goals. You slowly drown in distractions until you can no longer think, act, or be yourself. This explains the misery of most people."

### Common Sense Observation
**Instructions:** Point out overlooked truths and relate them to broader concepts.

**Example:** "One pattern I've noticed in happy people: They're obsessive about maintaining their mental clarity."

### Comparison/Metaphor/Analogy
**Instructions:** Use numbers and percentages for attention. Provide perspective through comparison.

**Example:**
```
1% of people create content.
10% of people curate content.
89% of people consume content.

Which group do you think makes the most money online?
```

### Comparisons
**Instructions:** Present a stark contrast between two options. Encourage the reader to make a choice.

**Example:**
```
The right woman makes everything easier.
The wrong woman makes everything harder.

Choose yours wisely.
```

### Concept Comparison

```
A simple "thank you" can transform workplace culture and motivation.
```

### Time Management Truth
**Instructions:** Point out how time spent on one thing could be used for another.
**Format:** if you (have something valuable to do something not valuable)...

**Example:** "If you have time to consume, you have time to create."

### Transformation Journey
**Instructions:** Share a personal struggle, negative habits, examples, turning point, and valuable lesson.
**Format:** I struggle to (something I have done)...

**Example:** "I struggled to make consistent money online. I was always looking for shortcuts. Crypto. Trading. Matched betting. But then I focused on learning to write. 6 months later… I'm doing it for a living. It's hard to go wrong if you focus on gaining valuable skills."

### Transformative Solution
**Instructions:** Pinpoint a singular, powerful action or decision that can lead to significant life changes.
**Format:** "_ is the only solution to change your life."

**Example:** "Taking responsibility is the only solution to change your life."

### Ultimate Remedy
**Instructions:** Describe a non-medical solution that has powerful healing effects on one's life.
**Format:** "_ is the greatest medicine"

**Example:** "Laughter is the greatest medicine"

### Unconventional Opinion
**Instructions:** Present a controversial view and invite engagement.
**Format:** "Unpopular Opinion: {Spicy/unconventional statement} Agree or not?"

**Example:** "Unpopular Opinion: College degrees are becoming less valuable. Agree or not?"

### Underrated Skill Identification
**Instructions:** Highlight an often overlooked but crucial ability.
**Format:** "The most underrated skill in life is _."

**Example:** "The most underrated skill in life is active listening."

### Unexpected Influence
**Instructions:** Share an experience with someone who influences you to try new things.
**Format:** I'm on a (experience with someone who does something)...

**Example:** "I'm on a date with a girl who runs marathons. I don't run often. But yesterday we ran 3km, dived into a river, and ran back. Then she started smashing out sit-ups... So I joined in. It really shows... \"You can get rich just by being around the right people.\" - D.L."

### Unique Discovery
**Instructions:** Share personal insights framed as valuable realizations.

**Example:** "The greatest skill one can develop is decreasing the time between idea and execution."

### Uniqueness Affirmation
**Instructions:** Acknowledge others' strengths while emphasizing personal uniqueness.
**Format:** "Someone will always be _. But they will never be you"

**Example:** "Someone will always be smarter. But they will never be you"

### Universal Requirement
**Instructions:** Choose a factor that's crucial for success in multiple important areas of life.
**Format:** "_ is needed to build both health and wealth"

**Example:** "Discipline is needed to build both health and wealth"

### Universal Success Ingredients
**Instructions:** Identify two elements that are present in every successful journey.
**Format:** "All paths to success have _ and _ in them"

**Example:** "All paths to success have struggle and growth in them"

### Value Hierarchy
**Instructions:** Identify a quality or skill that outweighs natural ability in importance.
**Format:** "_ is more important than talent"

**Example:** "Persistence is more important than talent"

### Value-Based Success
**Instructions:** Explain how prioritizing values led to success.
**Format:** I became (something great) because I didn't (do something others do)...

**Example:** "I became self-employed at 23 because I didn't play the status game. I drove a 2006 Corolla instead of a new car. I invested in mentors instead of a nice watch. Don't be the person who'd rather look the part instead of playing the part a couple of years later."

### Warning
**Instructions:** Caution against common mistakes. Explain consequences and offer alternatives.

**Example:** "Be careful telling people about your goals. It releases dopamine similar to achieving them. Don't tell people what you are going to do. Tell people what you are doing."

### Wealth Creation Contrast
**Instructions:** Contrast a common misconception about wealth creation with a more effective approach.
**Format:** "_ won't make you rich. _ will."

**Example:** "Working harder won't make you rich. Working smarter will."

### "What, How, Why Format"
**Instructions:** 
- State what to do
- Provide examples of how to do it
- Explain why it's important

**Example:**
```
Do what most people don't.

Go outside. Observe your thoughts. Go on a run. Go to the gym. Respect others. Groom yourself. Set goals. Study daily. Build daily. Take less value, give more.

It's not hard to stand out in a world that has normalized mediocrity.
```

# Content Proliferation Methods

Use these methods to turn 1 idea into 1000 variations.

---

## Method 1: SCAMPER

Generate 7 variations from one post (one per letter).

### S - Substitute
**What can you replace?**
- The main idea with an adjacent concept
- The method/process for accomplishing the benefit
- The examples while keeping the structure
- The angle or pain point

**Example:**

Original:
> "The most successful solopreneurs don't start with funding. They start with a customer."

Substitute:
> "The most successful solopreneurs don't start with a perfect product. They start with a paying client."

---

### C - Combine
**What can be combined?**
- Merge with personal experience or story
- Combine two similar concepts for more power
- Add a reply to someone else's post as your own content
- Combine data points for stronger evidence

**Example:**

Original:
> "The most successful solopreneurs don't start with funding. They start with a customer."

Combine:
> "I spent years thinking I needed VC funding to build a real business. Then I met Sarah Chen. She started with 1 client paying $500/month. Within a year, $15k MRR. The most successful founders don't start with funding. They start with a customer."

---

### A - Adapt
**What can be added?**
- Expand Heavy-Hitting One-Liner into full post or thread
- Add "missing piece" that wasn't in original
- Adapt to different platform with same core idea
- Add data, examples, or proof points

**Example:**

Original:
> "The most successful solopreneurs don't start with funding. They start with a customer."

Adapt (expand to thread):
> "Thread: 3 things successful solopreneurs start with (hint: not funding)
>
> 1. One paying customer (even at low price)
> 2. A skill people will pay for (not necessarily unique)
> 3. Time to deliver (10-15 hours/week minimum)
>
> That's it. The rest you figure out as you go."

---

### M - Modify
**What can be modified, maximized, magnified, or minimized?**
- Magnify specific pain points (exaggeration for effect)
- Use same idea in different post format
- Make more "punchy" by removing words
- Minimize complexity, maximize impact

**Example:**

Original:
> "The most successful solopreneurs don't start with funding. They start with a customer."

Modify (magnify pain):
> "You're waiting for the 'perfect' investor pitch while competitors are eating your lunch with scrappy MVPs. The most successful solopreneurs don't wait for funding. They start with a customer."

---

### P - Purpose (Put to other use)
**What other use or angle?**
- Angle for different audience segment
- What other benefit/pain point can this solve?
- Different context or application
- Different stage of journey (awareness vs action)

**Example:**

Original (for aspiring founders):
> "The most successful solopreneurs don't start with funding. They start with a customer."

Purpose (for burned-out employees):
> "Burned out employees: The most successful solopreneurs aren't serial entrepreneurs with connections. They're people who found one client willing to pay for their skills. You already have skills. You just need one paying customer."

---

### E - Eliminate
**What can be removed or simplified?**
- Remove words to make punchier
- Simplify complex explanations
- Cut nuance and uncertainty
- Reduce to core essence

**Example:**

Original:
> "The most successful solopreneurs don't start with funding. They start with a customer."

Eliminate:
> "Successful solopreneurs start with customers, not funding."

---

### R - Reverse
**What happens if you reverse the idea?**
- Flip positive to negative (or vice versa)
- "With X you get Y" becomes "Without X you stay stuck with Z"
- Reverse the order or flow
- Challenge the original premise

**Example:**

Original (positive):
> "The most successful solopreneurs don't start with funding. They start with a customer."

Reverse (negative):
> "Without customers, your funding means nothing. Ask any failed startup founder."

---

## Method 2: The 8 Human Desires

Spin concept through different desire lenses to create variations.

### The 8 Desires

1. **Safety of Tribe** - Belonging, protection, community
2. **Survival & Success** - Achievement, results, winning
3. **Life Enjoyment** - Fun, pleasure, experiences
4. **Social Acceptance** - Status, approval, fitting in
5. **Sexual Companionship** - Attraction, relationships (often not applicable)
6. **Comfort & Clarity** - Understanding, certainty, ease
7. **Freedom From Fear** - Overcoming obstacles, courage
8. **Perceived Status** - Recognition, reputation, respect

### How to Use

**Original Concept:** "Customers vs Funding"

**1. Safety of Tribe:**
> "Your business needs customers who get your vision. Not investors who want control. The most successful solopreneurs build customer relationships first, investor relationships never."

**2. Survival & Success:**
> "The path to success isn't through funding rounds. It's through paying customers. Every successful bootstrapped founder knows this. The VC-backed startups are failing. The customer-funded businesses are thriving."

**3. Life Enjoyment:**
> "Life's too short to spend it in pitch meetings. Successful solopreneurs prioritize serving customers over courting investors."

**4. Social Acceptance:**
> "Worried what people will think if you launch without funding? The most successful solopreneurs worried about that too. Then they did it anyway. Now they're the ones other founders want to be like."

**5. Sexual Companionship:**
> (Skip - not applicable for business content)

**6. Comfort & Clarity:**
> "Starting a business feels overwhelming. Too many pitch decks, too many investor meetings needed. Here's what brings clarity: successful solopreneurs don't start there. They start with 1 customer willing to pay. Everything else becomes clear after that."

**7. Freedom From Fear:**
> "The fear of 'not having enough capital' stops more businesses than anything else. But here's the truth: the most successful founders aren't the most funded. They're the ones who pushed through that fear and started with customers."

**8. Perceived Status:**
> "Society tells you funding = success. But successful solopreneurs know better. Real status comes from customers who love your product, revenue you actually own, and a business you built from scratch."

## Method 3: Vision/Anti-Vision Framing

Frame concepts through your vision (what you're FOR) and anti-vision (what you're AGAINST).

### Vision Example
- Autonomy and flexibility for creators
- Building real wealth, not inflated valuations
- Customers who love what you do
- Founders empowered, not beholden to investors
- Sustainable, profitable businesses

### Anti-Vision Example
- VC treadmill and burnout culture
- Institutional gatekeeping and credential requirements
- Businesses that lose money but "grow"
- Founders feeling trapped by investor demands
- Exit-or-bust mentality

### How to Use

**Original Concept:** "Customers vs Funding"

**Vision Framing (what you're FOR):**
> "This is what business freedom looks like: customers who pay you, work you control, growth at your pace. No investor meetings required. Just value delivery. That's how the most successful solopreneurs build. That's the path to real independence."

**Anti-Vision Framing (what you're AGAINST):**
> "The VC gatekeepers want you to believe you can't build something big without their money. They want you dependent on their check. The most successful solopreneurs ignored them and started with customers instead. That's how you break free from the funding treadmill."

**Both (Transformation):**
> "From investor dependency to customer freedom. From 'you need funding' to 'you need paying customers.' The most successful solopreneurs made this shift. You can too."

## Proliferation Workflow

### Step 1: Identify High Performers
- Monthly: Study top-performing posts
- Look for: Most followers, highest engagement, best conveys brand philosophy
- Save in separate document

### Step 2: Generate Variations
For each high performer:
- 7 SCAMPER variations
- 5-8 Human Desire reframes (skip non-applicable ones)
- 2-3 Vision/Anti-Vision frames
- **Total: 14-18 variations from 1 post**

### Step 3: Select & Schedule
- Pick best 3-5 variations
- Space out over weeks/months
- Test different angles with same core concept
- Track which variations perform best

### Step 4: Learn & Iterate
- Document which proliferation methods work best
- Note which Human Desires resonate most with audience
- Refine vision/anti-vision framing based on response

---

## When to Use Each Method

**SCAMPER:**
- Quick variations needed
- Same concept, different formats
- Adapting for different platforms
- Making content punchier

**Human Desires:**
- Reaching different audience segments
- Testing emotional angles
- Deepening connection with specific desire
- When original post feels flat

**Vision/Anti-Vision:**
- Brand alignment critical
- Transformation stories
- Challenging status quo
- Rallying call-to-action posts

**All Three:**
- Monthly proliferation of top performers
- High-volume content campaigns
- Testing and learning phase
- Building content library
