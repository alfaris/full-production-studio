# Human Writing

Transform AI-assisted drafts into authentic, distinctive prose that sounds like a real person wrote it.

## Core Principle

**Rewrite, don't patch.** When editing AI output, identify the core message and reconstruct from scratch rather than tweaking phrases.

## The #1 AI Tell

**The Correlative Construction:** "X isn't just Y - it's Z"

This is the default structure AI produces. Every AI sample uses it. Eliminate it completely.

- Bad: "Teachers aren't just instructors - they're mentors"
- Good: "Teachers mentor students."

## Forbidden Patterns

### Constructions
- "It's not about X, it's about Y"
- "The truth is..." / "The reality is..."
- "Now more than ever..."

### Rhetorical Flourishes
- "The best part? ..."
- "Here's the thing..."
- "Let's be honest..."
- "What if I told you..."

### Faux-Dramatic Fragments
- "No fluff. No filler. Just results."
- "Simple. Clear. Effective."

### Openers
- "In the ever-evolving world of..."
- "In today's fast-paced..."
- "Gone are the days when..."

### Formatting
- No boldface for emphasis in body text
- No emojis unless explicitly requested
- No "In conclusion" or "In summary"
- Use hyphens with spaces - like this - not em dashes

## Quick Scan Checklist

Before publishing, check for:
- [ ] Correlative constructions ("not just X, but Y")
- [ ] "Just" appearing more than once
- [ ] Hedge words (might, could, perhaps, possibly)
- [ ] Passive voice
- [ ] Vague claims without specifics
- [ ] Forbidden rhetorical flourishes

## Charlie's Signature Moves

1. **Aphoristic Opening** - Thesis as proverb
2. **Personal-to-Universal Bridge** - Start specific, expand, return with meaning
3. **Intellectual Name-Drop (Earned)** - Reference thinkers only when relevant
4. **Contrarian Hook** - Challenge received wisdom without edgelording
5. **Incarnational Detail** - Specific, sensory, grounding
6. **Self-Deprecating Confession** - Prevents preachiness, builds trust

## Target Tone

A coach who has done the thing and believes you can too. A friend who tells you the truth because he respects you. A fellow traveler, slightly ahead on the path.

# Forbidden Patterns (Complete Reference)

## Constructions

### Correlative Constructions (THE #1 AI TELL)
- "X aren't just Y - they're Z"
- "It's not about X, it's about Y"
- "More than just X, it's Y"

### Negation Structure
- "No X. No Y. Just Z."
- "No fluff. No theory. Just actionable insights."

### Other Faux-Profound Phrases
- "The truth is..." / "The reality is..."
- "Let that sink in"
- "Now more than ever..."

---

## Rhetorical Flourishes

### Short Hook Questions
- "The best part? ..."
- "The secret? ..."
- "Want access?"
- "Ready to level up?"
- "Sound familiar?"

### Setup Lines
- "Sounds impossible? It's not."
- "What if I told you..."
- "Here's the thing..."
- "Let's be honest..."
- "At the end of the day..."

### Transition Tells
- "And here's the kicker"
- "Enter: [thing]"
- "X changed everything"
- "This symbolizes..." / "Which reflects..."

---

## Staccato Phrasing

Do not use faux-dramatic sentence fragments:
- "No fluff. No filler. Just results."
- "Big ideas. Small words. Maximum impact."
- "Simple. Clear. Effective."
- Short. Choppy. Sentences. Like. This.

---

## Triple Threat Syndrome

AI groups everything in threes. Avoid:
- "Fast, efficient, reliable"
- "Bold, beautiful, brilliant"
- Any adjective triplet that feels too neat

---

## Openers

- "In the ever-evolving world of..."
- "In today's fast-paced..."
- "Gone are the days when..."
- "In an era where..."
- "Let's dive in..."

---

## Closers

- "In conclusion..."
- "In summary..."
- "To your success" (email sign-off)

---

## AI Vocabulary (Immediate Fail If Present)

### Intensity Words
- comprehensive, crucial, vital, essential
- seamless, cutting-edge, game-changing
- supercharge, skyrocket, unlock

### Action Words
- delve, dive into, unpack
- navigate, leverage, foster
- embark, journey (unless literal travel)

### Abstraction Words
- landscape, realm, sphere
- tapestry, myriad, multifaceted
- paradigm, framework (unless technical)

---

## Thesaurus Abuse

Don't use fancy words when simple words work:
- "utilize" → just say "use"
- "implement" → just say "start" or "do"
- "optimize" → just say "improve"
- "execute" → just say "do"
- "facilitate" → just say "help"
- "highlighting" → just say "show"

---

## Overused Words

### "Just" and "Actually"
- Bad: "We just need to understand that it's just about learning"
- Good: "It's about learning. Focus on what matters."
- Limit: Once per 500 words maximum for "actually"

### Hedging Stacks
Remove these:
- might / could / perhaps / possibly / maybe
- "may potentially possibly"
- "could perhaps"
- "might be able to"
- "It's worth considering..."
- "You might want to think about..."
- "It's important to note that..."
- "Some people say..." → "I've found that..."
- "It could be argued..." → "It's true that..."
- "One could say..." → "This is true because..."

Test: Do I actually believe this? If yes, remove the hedge.

---

## Empty Enthusiasm

- "Absolutely!"
- "Great question!"
- "You're absolutely right!"

---

## Authenticity Theater

- Overusing "real" ("real strategy from real experts getting real results")
- Overusing "actually" to sound casual
- Forced relatability ("we've all been there")

---

## Punctuation Overload

- **Em dash overload**: 6 em dashes per paragraph is a tell
- **Arrow overload**: → used more than once per piece

---

## Passive Voice

- Bad: "It was determined that improvements were needed"
- Good: "We need to improve"

Test: If you can't identify who's doing the action, rewrite it.

---

## Corporate Jargon

- Bad: "Leveraging synergies across stakeholder ecosystems"
- Good: "Getting different people talking to each other"

Test: Would you say this to a friend?

---

## Vague Language

- Bad: "Our users improve"
- Good: "Users improve by 40% in 6 months"

Test: Can you back it up with a specific number, example, or name?

---

## Transitions

Limit transitions to: but, and, so, then, because

Remove: furthermore, moreover, additionally, it's important to note that...

---

## Formatting Rules

- No boldface for emphasis in body text
- No emojis unless explicitly requested
- No summaries like "In conclusion" or "In summary"
- Use hyphens with spaces - like this - not em dashes

---

## The Core Test

> "If I removed the byline and showed this to someone, would they guess AI or human?"

When in doubt: **Does this have fingerprints on it?**

# The Humanization Workflow

Step-by-step process for transforming AI drafts into authentic prose.

## Step 1: Understand Your Source

- What's the core insight or story?
- What's the emotional arc?
- What's the 30-second version?

## Step 2: Apply SUCKS Framework

- Who is my ONE reader?
- Is this unique and useful?
- Clear, curious, conversational?
- Simple and structured?
- What will stick?

## Step 3: Write or Edit

If editing AI output: **Rewrite rather than patch.** Identify the core message and reconstruct.

## Step 4: Apply Sticky Sentences

Identify 2-3 most important statements. Add alliteration, symmetry, contrast, or rhythm.

## Step 5: Eliminate AI Tells

Scan systematically:
- [ ] Correlative constructions
- [ ] Overuse of "just" and "actually"
- [ ] Hedge words
- [ ] Passive voice
- [ ] Corporate jargon
- [ ] Vague language
- [ ] Forbidden patterns
- [ ] Too many transitions

## Step 6: Read Aloud

- Does it sound like a real person talking?
- Does energy transfer (conversation, not lecture)?
- Is every sentence earning its place?
- Are there obvious AI tells remaining?

## The Final Test

1. Does it tell a story that resolves?
2. Does it give the reader something actionable?
3. Does it sound like Charlie Deist wrote it?
4. Would I want to read the next section?
5. **Would this sentence appear in a ChatGPT output? If yes, rewrite it.**

## Charlie's Signature Moves

1. **Aphoristic Opening** - Thesis statements disguised as proverbs
   - "A vigorous life is the best training for a 50-mile march, and a 50-mile march is the best training for a vigorous life."

2. **Personal-to-Universal Bridge**
   - Start with a specific personal moment
   - Expand to cultural/historical commentary
   - Return to the personal with new meaning

3. **Intellectual Name-Drop (Earned)** - Reference thinkers (Ivan Illich, Ray Peat, Georges Hebert) only when directly relevant. Always explain why.

4. **Contrarian Hook** - Challenge received wisdom without becoming edgelord
   - "In 99% of cases of chronic sedentarism, the inactive person is not lazy. They're bored."

5. **Incarnational Detail** - Specific, sensory, grounding
   - RFK walking in "leather Oxford dress shoes"

6. **Self-Deprecating Confession** - Prevents preachiness, builds trust
   - "I write this admonition as someone who often scrolls Twitter on the couch"

## Target Tone

```
[Too Soft] -------- [Target Zone] -------- [Too Hard]
     |                     |                     |
Self-help pablum    Earned authority      Drill sergeant
Passive voice       Active, direct        Contemptuous
Apologetic          Confident            Preachy
```

Target: A coach who has done the thing and believes you can too.

# Sticky Sentences

Techniques to create memorable statements.

## Alliteration

Same starting sounds:
- "Specificity is the secret"
- "The best jobs are neither decreed nor degreed"

## Symmetry

Parallel structure:
- "Read for awareness. Write for understanding."
- "It's not 10,000 hours. It's 10,000 iterations."

## Contrast

Opposing ideas:
- "To be everywhere is to be nowhere."
- "Be clear, not clever. Concise, not complex."

## Rhyme

Similar ending sounds:
- "Tell a story or lose your glory"

## Rhythm

Pleasing cadence:
- "When you can't wait to share it, they can't help but read it"

## Combinations

For maximum stickiness, combine techniques:
- "Anger prepares us to fight. Fear prepares us to flee." (Symmetry + Alliteration)

## Application

1. Identify 2-3 most important statements in your piece
2. Try each technique on each statement
3. Pick the one that sounds most natural
4. Don't force it - if nothing works, clear prose is better than awkward stickiness

# The SUCKS Framework

Before writing anything, answer these questions.

## S - Specific

- Write for ONE person, not a crowd
- If you write for everyone, you write for no one

## U - Unique & Useful

- Does this change how your reader thinks, feels, or acts?

## C - Clear, Curious, Conversational

- Main idea is unmissable
- Reads like talking to a friend

## K - Kept Simple & Structured

- Simple ideas, simple words
- Clear beginning, middle, end

## S - Sticky

- Memorable phrases they'll repeat

## Pre-Flight Checklist

- [ ] Who is my ONE reader?
- [ ] Is this unique and useful?
- [ ] Clear, curious, conversational?
- [ ] Simple and structured?
- [ ] What will stick?
